# Opposition to the integrated reconstruction v5

13 October 2026. Separate nonblinded automated review of `reports/integrated-reconstruction-v5.md`, read completely. Exact deployment/configuration unavailable. This reviewer supplied the preceding constitution assessment; agreement with that assessment is not independent evidence, and the objections below also correct its overextended implications. No report edits were made.

**Verdict:** the draft establishes the failure of a strong grounding inference and a documented qualification of explanatory completeness. It does **not yet establish actual underdetermination of person assignment from Hume's full empirical evidence**, nor identify assignment as the Appendix's precise difficulty. Those two conclusions must be distinguished from the successful countermodel. The surviving positive proposal is a method for exposing conclusion-specific dependencies; a completed account of warranted ownership has not been supplied.

## 1. The graph result does not establish the alleged empirical ownership deficit

**Target wording:** “The consequential negative result concerns a proposed foundation: causal regularity alone cannot both supply the units of inquiry and establish what those units ultimately are.” Later: “Assignment and causal connectedness are different premises,” followed by the practical consequence for other-minds inference.

The formal result is sound for an **unlabelled edge structure**: one chain can be partitioned into personal histories in multiple ways. But Hume does not infer personal ownership from that abstraction alone. T2.1.11 uses a countenance, conversation, kinship, acquaintance, location and contrasts between particular people. T1.4.6 adds memory and a first-person train; .18 starts with a hypothetical view into someone else's already characterized succession. Those observations and predicates are not held fixed by retaining only the four graph edges.

Accordingly, the countermodel shows a failure of `bare C -> Owner`. It does not show a failure of `C + body/history/recognition/context evidence -> ordinary Owner`, nor that the relevant contextual evidence itself must assert a real composite substance. Treating the former as the latter would make underdetermination an artifact of discarding the very information the explanation uses. A causal relation can also be represented with already indexed relata; identifying the predicates' semantic requirements does not establish an additional unmet evidential requirement.

T2.1.11.16–17 is a good test of why indexing matters: sympathy varies when relevant relations concern the same people rather than different people. It is not itself evidence that Hume cannot discriminate those people or that the discrimination needs a metaphysical unity premise. The draft's acknowledgement of joint tracking does not completely repair the introductory universal claim.

**Suggested replacement:** “A bare causal edge structure does not determine its partition into personal histories. Hume's richer descriptions already distinguish persons and their signs; the reconstruction must state what supplies that indexing before claiming that causation itself explains it. This exposes an explanatory dependency, not demonstrated underdetermination from all the evidence Hume uses.”

This objection also applies to my preceding note's strongest rhetoric about owner assignments changing while the causal graph stays fixed. The graph test remains valid; the move to a defect in Hume's complete evidence is withdrawn unless a richer counterexample is supplied.

## 2. Present episode attribution and continuing personal history are different targets

**Target wording:** “Inferring pain from a kind of sign differs from assigning that episode to a continuing personal history. The latter changes character estimates and moral judgments.”

This is useful, but it runs together three propositions: an episode occurs; it is the pain of this presently identified patient; and it belongs to the same continuing history as an earlier episode. The signs in T2.1.11.3/.8 and T3.3.1.7 do not begin as evidence for wholly unowned pain and then add a person in a second step. The person-indexed content is already in the represented passion and its signs. By contrast, the diachronic conclusion can require further evidence. The report should not suggest that every ordinary present attribution imports strict persistence or the complete constitution of consciousness.

The stable-character criticism is valid against a rule making unchanged character necessary for ordinary identity: T1.4.6.19 explicitly permits change. It is not a criticism of all use of character to identify or track someone. A temporarily stable trait can be evidence without being a necessary condition, and histories can be revised jointly with character estimates. The report already recognizes joint tracking; its stronger implication must be restricted to the stated sole-basis rule.

**Suggested change:** separate `episode`, `present patient`, `continuing history`, `strict identity`, and `one consciousness` in the model. Add at least one test in which evidence identifies the present patient while failing to establish the diachronic extension. Otherwise “the latter changes moral judgment” remains a logical possibility rather than a demonstrated Humean consequence.

## 3. The Appendix's precise diagnosis is still an interpretation

**Target wording:** “the retained causal account cannot be advertised as already solving the condition of unified ownership whose explanation the Appendix finds defective.”

Appendix20–21 clearly announces a defective explanation of the union of perceptions in thought or consciousness. The passage also describes reflected ideas introducing one another. It does not explicitly state a problem of assigning episodes to owners, a failure to distinguish my perceptions from yours, or a modern boundary condition for causal networks. The difficulty could concern how a reflective thought connects independent existences, how an appearance of identity is generated, or whether a single consciousness can be accounted for at all. These are competing diagnoses, not synonymous formulations.

The formal point that the two listed principles are satisfiable by themselves is valuable. It makes an additional explanatory demand visible; it cannot select **which** demand Hume failed to reconcile. “Ownership” is therefore a candidate interpretation of the structural problem. It is too strong to present it as the explicit Appendix target while the personal-identity literature remains incompletely reconstructed.

**Suggested replacement:** “The retained causal-belief account is not presented as a satisfactory explanation of the union of successive perceptions in thought or consciousness. Reading the admitted failure as a problem of episode ownership is one proposed diagnosis; the text does not settle that diagnosis against rival readings.”

## 4. Development concerns explanatory confidence; no changed constitutive premise has been established

**Target heading:** “The Appendix changes a dependency, not everything.”

The actual chronology is responsibly qualified elsewhere. But the heading can imply that Hume changed an inferential premise. The strongest demonstrated change is his assessment of the adequacy/consistency of the former explanation: T1.4.6.16 had treated the reasoning as decisive, whereas Appendix10/20–21 admits failure while retaining important premises. That is already consequential. The source does not identify a replacement premise or a repaired dependency.

**Suggested change:** heading “The Appendix withdraws confidence in explanatory completion.” Give the model an explicit distinction between a retained proposition, withdrawn confidence in its explanation, and a new explanatory premise. Mark our consequence — that the causal theory cannot be assumed to complete the constitutive theory — as derived, rather than a documented new doctrine. Book3's co-publication is correctly treated as coexistence; maintain the composition-order limitation.

The retained causal-belief discussion supports a **negative limit on a global-retreat reading**. It does not by itself establish that those beliefs retain precisely the epistemic warrant or reflective authority the earlier model assigned them. The draft generally respects this point; it should retain it wherever “preserves” and “warrant” occur.

## 5. The positive method needs a substantive limiting principle

“Conclusion-specific evidential constraint” is a sound principle of argument reconstruction: specify a conclusion and identify its premises. In that unrestricted form, however, it is compatible with almost every rival interpretation. It can redescribe the bearer, ownership, assent and theological problems without explaining why Hume permits particular empirical extensions and limits others. The Xenophon case proves a distinct target-specific inference; it does not alone establish a general Humean standard of evidence.

The draft should state an earned, narrower positive rule: **when independent experience supplies relevant features of a known kind, a new instance may be inferred within the scope of those features; where a cause is characterized only through one effect, further attributes and predictions require additional evidence.** That rule is connected to EHU11's familiar-artificer contrast, rather than to a generic obligation to reveal premises. It remains defeasible and leaves authority open. Show exactly how present other-minds attribution satisfies the known-kind condition, and where personal-history extension introduces additional data. The report's religious section contains the material for this; connect it to the revised central claim rather than letting a generic method carry the positive result.

The person-indexing result could then be a consequence test of that rule instead of its sole proposed philosophical novelty. If richer Humean evidence suffices for ordinary indexing, that is a positive result; it should not be discounted merely because indexing was not derived from the unlabelled graph.

## 6. The strongest rivals are narrower than M1/M2

M1 combines empirical success→ontology, organization→one being and affirmative judgment→endorsement. M2 combines metaphysical uncertainty with the defeat of every unobserved-object inference. The report explicitly allows scholars to reject these bridges, which is good. Yet testing these constructed conjunctions is not by itself a substantive objection to the strongest Hakkarainen, Baxter or Garrett account. All three may reject parts of the combinations while retaining their main interpretation.

Label M1 and M2 as **internally constructed maximal hypotheses**, with links to the precise earlier HPA claims they replace. The actual philosophical comparisons are Hakkarainen versus Baxter on the domain/reality of compounds, and Garrett versus Baxter on probability/endorsement. The report's strongest disagreement analysis is in those sections. Do not let success against the maximal models imply a changed verdict on the accessible originals or a completed resolution of their dispute.

## Priority of repairs

Before publication, repair objections 1–3 wherever their stronger wording appears in the report or model. These affect the main candidate contribution. Objection4 is a chronological/status clarification. Objections5–6 determine how substantial the surviving positive argument is and what counts as opposition to a published rival.

The correction should not conceal the failed reconstruction. The record should state: the unrestricted graph countermodel survives; actual empirical ownership underdetermination is unestablished; the Appendix's constitutive failure is documented; identifying it specifically with ownership remains a hypothesis. The next informative result would be a richer evidence-preserving counterexample or an account of how Hume's non-substantial tracking practices supply ordinary indexing. Either could overturn the present ownership diagnosis.
