# Positive reconstruction: an experienced mental type can support an unobserved instance

Assessment by the separate Treatise investigator, 12 October 2026. This is an original reconstruction from public primary passages, not a claim to have discovered an unprecedented argument. Exact deployment and configuration are unavailable; model identity is not independently inferred.

**Result.** There is substantial evidence for a principled distinction between inferring another creature's passions from signs and inferring wholly unexperienced material archetypes from perceptions. The distinction concerns the empirical availability of the causal types being compared. It supports Hume's ordinary and scientific analogical warrant. It does not prove other consciousness against every conceivable alternative, establish a mental substance, or vindicate an inference to mind-independent matter.

## The positive argument

1. A passion or purposive mental operation is an experienced kind of event. In one's own case, an intention, its circumstances, and ensuing conduct can be encountered together. This gives the proposed mental cause content independently of the particular stranger's behaviour being explained. It supplies many occurrences of a relation, although not many independently inspected subjects.
2. The stranger's signs and circumstances resemble relevant members of those experienced patterns. Transfer to another instance is therefore an analogy between event types already available to experience. It is an inference, not immediate access to that stranger's experience. Its warrant increases with relevant resemblance, varied observations, and successful discrimination among circumstances.
3. This is precisely the starting point of [T1.3.16.2–3](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/3/16), SBN176–177: consciousness of our own purposive actions precedes the inference from similar outward actions in other creatures to similar internal operations. The section subsequently uses learned behaviour and changes of training as a test of psychological explanation (.5–8), rather than treating purposive appearance alone as sufficient for every specific attribution.
4. By contrast, the postulated material archetype of a perception has never occurred as one member of a separately inspected archetype–perception pair. [T1.4.2.47](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/2), SBN212, and [EHU12.11–14](https://davidhume.org/texts/e/12), SBN152–154, explicitly identify that missing experience. Neither token absence nor invisibility by itself is the defect: the defect is the absent basis for the proposed causal comparison.
5. An inference to another mental episode therefore has an empirical basis unavailable to that particular archetype inference. It can be warranted by Hume's standards without an exception to causal reasoning, provided it claims a sufficiently similar experienced kind of episode, with calibrated probability. This conclusion does not require perceiving the productive necessity of either relation.

The most useful later support is [EHU11.24–27 and .30](https://davidhume.org/texts/e/11), SBN142–148. The narrator offers the unfinished building and footprint; the friend distinguishes inference about humans because additional experience of the species provides evidence beyond the single effect. The narrator's concluding .30 also requires experienced similarity on both sides of a causal inference. The difference is evidential, not a licence obtained merely by changing conversational setting. These are dialogue positions and cannot all be attributed to Hume without qualification; their common empirical restriction is also independently supported by [EHU9.1](https://davidhume.org/texts/e/9), SBN104–105, which grades analogical strength by resemblance.

## A concrete test, and the strongest failure condition

Suppose someone withdraws a hand near heat. The candidate attribution of pain uses an experienced relation among heat, one's own pain, and withdrawal. It gains support from responses to intensity, avoidance, later caution, and the wider pattern of conduct. A person performing a scene may reproduce one sign without the presumed passion; a composed sufferer may lack the sorrow an observer feels. These cases require attention to circumstances and competing explanations. They do not make social inquiry equivalent to inventing a cause whose kind has never been experienced.

The strongest objection occurs at step2: bodily or behavioural similarity does not independently show that consciousness accompanies those signs in another subject. All target evidence remains available to the observer through the observer's own perceptions. Subsequent successful predictions corroborate a model of conduct; they do not uniquely exclude a behaviourally indistinguishable creature without experience. Moreover, testimony cannot initially supply independent mental cases if its status as another person's report is exactly what is being established. The shared mental nature in T2.1.11.5 is thus not an independently observed premise that may simply be inserted to defeat this objection.

**Failure condition:** if the required conclusion is a non-circular proof of other consciousness from premises acceptable to the radical sceptic, or a uniquely identified necessary mental cause, the reconstruction fails. If the conclusion is defeasible analogical entitlement within Hume's empirical practice, T1.3.16 provides explicit support. The own-case analogy supplies an available cause type rather than presupposing the target episode, but the transfer is still inductive and cannot demonstrate the uniformity that makes it persuasive. This is a substantive limit of the solution, not an omission to conceal with an appeal to common life.

The objection that only one mind is directly inspected is therefore substantial but does not by itself defeat Hume's account. One subject can provide repeated episodes; [EHU9, note H.3](https://davidhume.org/texts/e/9), SBN107, even allows an accurate single experiment some inferential force through the general habit of transferring the known to the unknown. This provides a Humean answer to a blanket sample-size prohibition. It supplies no independent proof that the bodily similarities are sufficient for mental similarity, and it should not be mistaken for observing millions of other consciousnesses.

There is also an important scope correction. Some unobserved physical causes can be inferred from experienced kinds: the obstruction in the clock at EHU8.13 is an example. Conversely, mental productive powers remain inaccessible. The supported division is therefore **experienced causal comparison versus unsupported extrapolation**, not social causes versus all material causes. Another mind's independence from this observer is compatible with its being a mental episode; it does not entail the existence of matter independent of every mind.

## Chronology, access, and precedent

Treatise Books 1–2 were published in 1739; Book 3 in 1740. The Enquiry first appeared in 1748 and underwent later revision. The public [Treatise witness](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/notes) incorporates errata, Appendix additions and editorial interventions; the [Enquiry witness](https://davidhume.org/texts/e/notes) follows 1777. Paragraph and SBN references above are verified against those online texts, not independently inspected page facsimiles. No assertion is made that every 1777 clause appeared unchanged in 1748.

Complete sections read for this assessment: T1.3.16; T2.1.11; T2.2.5; T2.2.7 including its note; T2.2.9; T3.3.1; EHU8 including notes, EHU9 including note H, EHU11 including note M. Countertext coverage is selective: T1.4.2.39–57, EHU7.6–20 (plus the opening of .21), EHU12.1–16. These bounds prevent a claim to have examined every relevant passage across Hume's works.
