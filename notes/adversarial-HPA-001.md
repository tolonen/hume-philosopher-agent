## Defence of the target

Millican’s strongest local case is dialectical. At pp. 698–699 the opponent’s proposed extra material necessity would defeat equivalence with mental necessity; the definitions must therefore restrict coherent competing content. Note 61 rejects normative symmetry as sufficient. In 2011, pp. 141–142, he conditionally allows a reconciliation if the subjective idea determinately represented thick necessity, locating the remaining objection in the representation account. These are limits on what the local argument establishes, not an unconditional refutation of every logically possible realism.

The primary challenge is correspondingly stronger than a prohibition on inconsistent terminology. T 1.3.14.33 rejects the physical/moral distinction’s foundation in nature. T 2.3.1.4 denies material necessity beyond the specified circumstances. Abstract 34 requires an alternative idea and declares that requirement impossible. The defender can therefore ask the alternative to retain those asserted conclusions, rather than substitute agnosticism about two underlying relations. The theological concession in T 2.3.2.4 answers the danger objection introduced immediately before it; it does not by itself repeal the main claim. Unknown qualities in T 1.3.14.27 are conceded while their identification with an intelligible external connexion is challenged.

## Weakest step in the objection and positive alternative

The weakest step is moving from a **logically possible semantic model** to a **Humean representation model**. One concept can certainly denote a feature whose nature it does not exhaust. But that familiar logical fact does not establish that this particular impression-derived idea represents a further causal feature. Nor does shared reference alone close the space for kinds of that feature varying between material and voluntary action. A type can admit materially different subtypes. The realist must explain why those differences cannot be the very physical/moral distinction Hume rejects.

Two different repairs should remain distinct. One repair excludes any conceptual basis for the proposed distinction and therefore preserves Hume’s asserted equivalence; it needs primary evidence for the restricted reference relation. Another repair says the distinction is unwarranted because empirical enquiry applies common rules; that preserves a methodological symmetry but attenuates the positive assertion about nature. Neither is impossible, but neither follows from compatibility alone. The dossier correctly recognises this burden. Its verdict should not be described as proving that only a reductive semantics could possibly meet it, because the 2011 condition explicitly makes room for a stronger alternative.

## Result

The unsuccessful-objection verdict survives separate assessment. No misquotation, ignored concession or defeated inference has been established. The best defence relies on the opponent’s particular distinction and the strength of Hume’s positive conclusion. The alternative remains a contestable reconstruction with a representation-theoretic debt; generic sceptical modesty does not pay that debt.

A useful discriminating next question is whether any primary passage licenses an idea of necessity to refer to an unknown causal feature while excluding the rival material/mental distinction. That would be new evidence relevant to the conditional opening, not permission to repeat the present candidate indefinitely. The review supplies no novelty judgment beyond the dossier’s already-published-precedent classification.
