**Verdict:** retain a qualified positive reconstruction; withdraw the mental/material version of the proposed boundary. The reconstruction distinguishes inferences among experienced kinds from an inference to specifically different, unexperienced ultimate bearers. It can explain how Hume affirms other people's mental processes while leaving a metaphysical question unsettled. It does not establish that he actually suspends judgment on that question, defeat ordinary bodily realism, or refute perspectivalism. The other-minds analogy itself is already published.

## Target and exact scope

The parent investigation tests whether positive human science by itself requires the metaphysical Realism disputed between Zahn and Hakkarainen. That published target, its qualifications, and the earlier unsuccessful objections are documented in [the preceding assessment](perspectivalism-adversarial-v2.md). This note tests a further positive interpretation, not a new attribution of an unqualified claim to either scholar.

Keep four propositions separate:

1. Someone else's feeling exists when I do not observe it.
2. That feeling is a different token of an experienced kind of mental event.
3. The feeling inheres in a particular kind of substance.
4. Sensory appearances originate in entities ontologically and causally independent of human perceptions.

The inference from 1 to 3 or 4 needs an additional premise. An event's independence from **my observation** does not make it independent of **all human perception**. A belief about someone else's pain nevertheless has existential content: it is not merely a belief that my current experience has a certain pattern. Nor does distinguishing these claims prove the truth or warrant of 1.

The narrower question is whether Hume's accepted causal practice supplies grounds for 1–2 without settling 3–4. The reconstruction below answers yes conditionally on that practice. Its epistemic standing under unrestricted sceptical reflection remains contested.

## Published precedent and the remaining novelty question

Byoungjae Kim explicitly reconstructs the own-case analogy: “I infer the ideas of the mental states in others” (article p. 9). Pages 9–10 use causal generalization; pp. 10–17 address perspective asymmetry and vivacity. His pp. 13–14 associate this account with external-world and identity belief rather than establishing our proposed contrast. The complete article, including its qualifications, was read. Thus the basic analogy cannot be presented as our discovery. [Kim, *Hume on the Problem of Other Minds*, online 2018, issue 2019](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/328822026_Hume_on_the_Problem_of_Other_Minds).

An earlier full argument also matters. Inoue examines the relation between affection and behaviour at the level of general types (pp. 80–82), but favours a person-perception account of sympathy (pp. 75–77, 83–86). This is further evidence that the field has already investigated the proposed starting point. [Inoue, *Hume's Position Concerning Other Minds* (2003), pp. 75–86](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jafpos1956/11/2/11_2_75/_pdf/-char/en).

Pitson, Vitz, and Waldow were located, but their relevant complete original arguments were not accessible in this bounded assessment. Kim's reports of them are discovery leads, not grounds for adjudicating their views. In particular, the accessible preview/snippets of Waldow's book do not authorize a verdict about its proposed solution or its treatment of perception ontology. O'Brien's 2025 article was also located, but its full-text routes failed. These are material gaps in any novelty claim.

What remains potentially useful is the explicit combination of causal generalization, the substance/cause distinction, and a carefully specified metaphysical target in the current disagreement. No systematic priority claim is established for that combination.

## Reconstructable positive argument

**P1, textual starting point.** Hume treats experience of thought, passion, action, and their relations as material for causal inquiry. T 1.3.16.2–3 does more than report an irresistible habit: it employs the inference in distinguishing acceptable and unacceptable explanations of mental operations. T 1.4.5.30 makes bodily causation part of this inquiry.

**P2, interpretive premise.** An inference may extend a known kind of causal relation to a previously unobserved token without a prior direct observation of that very token. This is a restrained application of T 1.3.15.6. Otherwise ordinary causal inference would be impossible. The controversial issue is whether the self/other difference defeats the claimed similarity, not whether the particular inferred event was already observed.

**P3, interpretive premise.** Another human being's passion may be construed as another instance of a familiar mental kind, associated with comparable conduct and circumstances. This does not require positing a specifically different nonperceptual substance. The premise allows perceptual and conceptual access to differ between self and other; it does not erase that difference.

**P4, textual restriction.** The perception–archetype inference criticized at T 1.4.2.47 lacks the experienced conjunction required for that particular causal inference. The problem is the proposed relation's evidential basis, not merely that its conclusion concerns something currently unobserved.

**Inference.** If P2–P3 are accepted within Hume's empirical practice, grounds for inferring another human mental event need not be grounds for identifying an ultimate, perception-independent bearer or origin of sensory appearances. A claim about causal relations does not silently inherit a claim about substance.

**Precise conclusion.** Empirical commitment to other people's mental events can be compatible with leaving the specified metaphysical question undecided. Positive human science therefore needs more than its own existence to settle that question. This supplies a substantive answer to the other-minds pressure on restricted suspension; it does not establish restricted suspension as Hume's actual final position.

The reconstruction has genuine content. It permits mistaken ascriptions to be corrected through surrounding causes, subsequent conduct, stable character, and conflicting evidence. It also permits empirical bodily causes. It withholds a further metaphysical conclusion when no adequate inference to that conclusion has been supplied. These permissions and restrictions are within one inquiry; switching psychological perspectives is not required to state them consistently.

## Strongest objections and replies

**The access asymmetry remains.** Observing my anger together with my conduct does not give me an observed pairing of *someone else's* anger and conduct. The cautious reply is that general causal inference concerns comparable kinds, not antecedently known tokens. But that only relocates the burden: which differences matter to the comparison? Deception, acting, differences in bodily expression, and one's limited own-case sample are real defeaters. A repaired argument allows uncertainty and multiple evidential constraints. It must not infer unrestricted knowledge of other minds from mere conceivability of a similar feeling.

A proposed shortcut fails: separability of a perception from a substantial self does not by itself establish symmetry between first-person and third-person access. The metaphysical possibility of an independent event and the manner in which its occurrence is known are different questions. Likewise, an impression and its copied idea may share a kind of phenomenal content without beliefs about two different people's pain sharing their complete relational content. These are objections to a route through the asymmetry, not a refutation of all analogical inference. See Kim's pp. 10–17 and note 4 for the original argument and its own acknowledged pressure point.

**Sympathetic response is not verification.** An affect produced by imagining another's pain can occur with mistaken attribution or fiction. Therefore the produced affect cannot alone establish a real corresponding event in another person. This matters to any proposed solution that lets the conversion into an impression do all the epistemic work. It does not show that sympathetic participation supplies no evidence, or that an inferential stage must always be consciously articulated. The primary text's distinction between idea formation and affective conversion is useful here.

**The realist can accept the distinction.** This is the strongest reply. Ordinary claims about bodies, injuries, voices, and material processes also involve familiar causal kinds and unobserved tokens. Hume's T 1.4.5.30 expressly helps the realist. The reconstruction cannot permit another person's pain while excluding ordinary bodily causes merely because the latter are material. It should concede such empirical commitments and insist only that they do not settle the further substance/origin question. A realist who claims only those ordinary commitments has not been opposed. A realist who claims that these entail the narrowly specified metaphysical thesis must supply the extra premise.

There is a further ontological cost: other people’s experiences are real additional events, not just my representations of them. An account that confines all existence to my present perceptions cannot use this reconstruction unchanged. Conversely, a pluralism of human perceptual events need not include nonperceptual substances. The argument preserves this option; it does not prove that the option is Hume's ontology. If the disputed independence is expressly indexed to *human* perception, animal mental events require a separate classification. The animal passage supplies a model of inference; it should not decide that taxonomy without argument.

**The perspectivalist can also accept local norms.** The distinction could operate within ordinary philosophical inquiry while its authority fails under unrestricted reflection. Nothing here answers the regress of T 1.4.1 or all the conflicts of T 1.4.7. For a stronger anti-perspectivalist result, one would have to establish that Hume retains this content restriction as an authoritative answer *after* the relevant sceptical reflection, not merely when inquiry resumes. That has not been demonstrated. The surviving contribution is a possible explanation of the science/metaphysics relation, not a global account of doxastic normativity.

**Unknown substance is not denied substance.** T 1.4.5 supports separating the questions, but ignorance of a bearer does not entail denying it or suspending all belief that there is one. A relative conception may remain possible. The present argument contests a claimed inferential entailment; it must not substitute an argument from silence for positive suspension.

## Later evidence: the Enquiry's restriction on extrapolation

E 11.24–30, SBN 142–148, supplies a particularly close later parallel. The dialogue allows inference from a human product to its maker and then to further effects because the cause belongs to an independently familiar kind. The friend's restriction in .25–27 must be identified as a character's speech. The narrator nevertheless restates the pertinent requirement in .30: “both the effect and cause must bear a similarity and resemblance to other effects and causes, which we know”. This makes the distinction more than an isolated concession to the friend. [First Enquiry, section 11](https://davidhume.org/texts/e/11).

Its bearing is limited but important. The empirical basis must support the *particular added attribution*, rather than an imagined cause authorizing further conclusions without new warrant. This fits a difference between knowledge of human causal regularities and speculation about an unfamiliar ultimate source. It is later evidence and cannot automatically determine the Treatise. Nor does section 11 offer a foundational solution to other minds: familiar human minds are already part of its accepted empirical starting point.

E 12.7–12, SBN 151–153, also distinguishes natural belief in an external universe from the evidential problem about sensory representations and their supposed originals. Reading these sections together sharpens the content of the question; it does not erase the contrast between natural assent and philosophical justification. [First Enquiry, section 12](https://davidhume.org/texts/e/12).

## Decision and next discriminant

Promote this only as a **contestable positive reconstruction supporting a limited failure of entailment**. Withdraw any formulation that declares material causes inaccessible as a class, identifies analogy as a new discovery, or claims a conclusive solution of other-minds scepticism. Keep open both ordinary empirical realism and a perspectivalist account of the norms used here.

The most useful next discriminant is exact commitment: what proposition about perception independence is required by a particular Book 2 inference, and what passage licenses adding it? A successful demonstration must identify that proposition and show either its licensed derivation or its dispensability. Calling all scientific claims “realism”, or calling all unobservable tokens “metaphysical”, would conceal the disputed step. Full access to Waldow's treatment is necessary before claiming novelty or an advance over her solution.
