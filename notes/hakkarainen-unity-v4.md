# Hakkarainen: spatial constitution, personal identity, and causal warrant

## Exact target and reconstruction

At manuscript p. 2, the target thesis characterizes each spatial property of a perception of a body as a mereologically complex trope, “a particular being and a qualitatively simple property” (eight quoted scholarly words in this note).

Hakkarainen argues that determinate length, area, volume, and figure belong to a perceptual complex rather than an underlying substance. A property can have parts while presenting one determination; qualitative and mereological simplicity differ. Comparison permits selective attention to an already present property rather than creating it. His minimal line and square examples develop this account (pp. 8–13). Distance and location receive tentative extensions (pp. 14–16). Note xiii explicitly separates synchronic unity from diachronic identity (p. 18); notes ix–x bracket multimodal bodies and distinct perceptual spaces (pp. 17–18).

That reconstruction identifies the assessed 2015 thesis, not the stronger 2019 argument advertised in metadata.

## Strongest defence and a substantive test

Hume's [T1.2.3.4–5](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/2/3) gives the arrangement of colored points positive representational work: copying a spatial idea copies their disposition, and abstraction can retain that disposition across differences of color. T1.2.3.14 treats compound extension as requiring real constituents. These passages give a defender more than the claim that we cannot know an underlying substance. They support an experienced, determinate organization of constituents.

The strongest defence then uses [T1.1.7.17–18](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/1/7). The white globe/black globe/white cube comparison distinguishes aspects of a presentation without supplying separately existing accidents. Consequently, the objection that an inseparable spatial property must be an invented extra substance attacks the wrong structure. A determinate figure may be an aspect of a real complex; it need not be another object added to that complex. Neither the separability of individual points nor the absence of an underlying bearer immediately excludes this possibility.

The hardest remaining step is whether positive organization establishes **one composite being** rather than organized constituents. An ordered plurality can have a determinate configuration without an additional numerically single whole. T1.1.7's distinction between aspects and their presentation does not by itself establish a whole distinct from its parts. Conversely, [T1.2.2.3](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/2/2)'s restrictive treatment of unity cannot simply be generalized into an objection to every organized complex without explaining T1.2.3.14's compound extension. The test is whether the alternative can preserve Hume's literal commitments to compound extension and determinate figure while treating unity only as attribution. This is a live burden on both readings, not a refutation of Hakkarainen. The full 2019 reply is needed before adjudicating its answer. An ordered-plurality objection here is my reconstruction, not an attribution of Baxter's exact argument. Another HPA agent has separately read Baxter's 2015 substance paper; that source-based report must govern exact Baxter attribution. The disagreement over composite beings remains ontological and is not dissolved by distinguishing labels for unity and identity.

## Positive contribution to HPA-006

Three claims require different bridges:

1. **Constitution now:** perceived parts have a determinate spatial arrangement. Hakkarainen supplies a positive account of what a spatial property could be without a substance/attribute bearer.
2. **Identity through change:** a changing collection continues as one person or body. [T1.4.6.8–12](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/6) distinguishes strict identity from the familiar attribution supported by common purpose and causal interdependence. Present organization does not entail strict persistence.
3. **Causal warrant beyond present observation:** outward signs warrant an unobserved mental episode. The HPA-006 own-case analogy requires experienced mental/behavioral types and a defensible transfer; it does not require that those types are accidents of a known substance. Spatial constitution can therefore make the positive reconstruction ontologically less empty without supplying the transfer premise itself.

This distinction creates a useful further test. Inferring an unobserved mental episode, identifying a causal network of episodes, and assigning every episode to one continuing consciousness are different achievements. A successful causal prediction cannot alone show that one phenomenal subject contains the entire network. Nor does an observer's access to an organized visual field establish a shared perceptual space with another observer.

The strongest failure condition is [Appendix 20–21](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/app): explaining the relations among distinct perceptions has not yet explained their union in consciousness. Spatial arrangement cannot generally answer this problem because passions, sounds, and successive episodes are not all contiguous colored or tangible points. The distinction permits a bounded positive theory of experienced structures and causal expectations; it does **not** dissolve Hume's admitted difficulty. Appendix 4's use of a neighboring person's voice and unseen body parts also shows that ordinary inference can operate within the same work that acknowledges the unity problem, although coexistence is not a solution to that problem.

The resulting proposal is substantive but limited: investigate structured perceptual presentations and experienced causal regularities while treating the constitution of a single continuing consciousness as a further explanatory demand. The causal core could operate with organized pluralities or real composites; it does not choose between those ontologies. This avoids making either a substance theory or a solution to personal identity an unargued prerequisite of every local inference. It also prevents successful social prediction from being mistaken for proof of the ontology of composite minds. The synchronic/diachronic distinction is already explicit in Hakkarainen's note, not a discovery of this assessment.
