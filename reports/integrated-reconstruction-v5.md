# What causal inquiry can establish: inference, individuation and assent in Hume

Reconstruction cycle v5, 12–13 October 2026. Automated research experiment; no human philosophical endorsement or established priority. Earlier dossiers remain part of the experiment’s history. This report supersedes the stronger grounding implications of the earlier positive reconstruction.

## Question and result

Can a single account of causal inference explain Hume’s positive claims about bodies and other people, their composition and persistence, and the authority with which he accepts them? The previous HPA proposal made relevant experience of a causal kind do too much work. Such experience can constrain an inference without establishing the continuing objects, ownership of mental episodes, or reflective authority that an enlarged conclusion requires.

The replacement is **conclusion-specific evidential constraint**, with a substantive local rule: independent experience of a relevant relation in a known kind can support an unobserved instance within the scope of those experienced features; a cause characterized only through one effect cannot acquire further attributes or consequences from that characterization alone. “Known” here marks an operative classification within inquiry, not a faculty-independent proof of object ontology. An inference must specify its target, the evidence bearing on that target, and the additional premises needed to extend it. A narrative can warrant a claim about its author’s available concepts while failing to warrant the narrated event. Bodily signs can support a mental-episode attribution without establishing a single enduring consciousness. An effect can support a limited analogy without authorizing additional properties or a return inference to further effects. These contrasts are not one generic sceptical disclaimer: changing the target changes what needs to be established, and sometimes changes the verdict.

The consequential negative result concerns a proposed foundation: causal regularity alone cannot both supply the units of inquiry and establish what those units ultimately are. The positive result concerns an operative method: Hume often compares evidence relative to a specified target and limits extensions whose additional premises are unavailable. This does not yet settle Garrett’s and Baxter’s disagreement about rational endorsement. The distinction between a local evidential verdict and its ultimate authority is a question to argue, not a solution by definition.

## Rivals and their disputed premises

The [prospective design](../REORIENTATION-v5.md) fixed four models and seven consequences before further retrieval. Earlier exposure to many passages makes this a nonblinded investigation. M1 and M2 are internally constructed maximal hypotheses used to test earlier HPA extensions; they are not complete attributions of the strongest published rival positions.

**M1, foundational scientific realism**, infers independent objects, unified persons and reflective endorsement from successful human science. Its vulnerable conjunction contains three bridges: successful prediction establishes the ontology of its relata; organized interaction establishes one being; and affirmative corrective judgment establishes rational endorsement. A realist can decline these bridges and defend its conclusions separately. Our tests concern this strong foundation model, not every realism.

**M2, universal suspension or fiction**, makes metaphysical uncertainty defeat all warranted unobserved-object claims. It predicts too much if it converts Hume’s careful positive comparisons into indiscriminate abstention. A qualified passive-assent account survives this criticism: it can accommodate strong belief, elaborate theory and normative language. Baxter’s strongest position belongs to that qualified rival, not the caricature that Hume cannot say anything affirmative.

**M3, evidence-calibrated projection**, originally proposed that familiar causal kinds license otherwise unavailable extensions. It survives for some comparisons, but fails as a general derivation of continued objects or enduring minds. Its revised form is conclusion-specific constraint, with individuation and authority exposed as additional dependencies.

**M4, developmental revision**, predicts identifiable changed premises or new arguments after an earlier difficulty. Its rival is continuity with altered presentation, purpose or audience. The Appendix gives explicit qualification; omission from an Enquiry does not independently give it. These hypotheses can receive different verdicts for different arguments.

## Reconstructing the early argument before using later works

In the 1739 Book1, T1.3.8.1–14 explains a transition from present evidence to an absent item through acquired association and vivacity. The science of human nature uses such transitions. Paragraph .10’s all-belief formulation appears broader than the later account of bodily continuity, and .12 describes argumentative preference in terms of feeling. Restricting the earlier maxim to direct causal transitions is therefore an interpretation requiring defence, not an uncontested global axiom. The subsequent body sequence and the Abstract’s common sentiment are preserved as pressure on its scope. No composition sequence between these Book1 sections has been established. [T1.3.8](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/3/8). T1.4.5.29–33 permits bodily dispositions and thought to be causally compared without explaining their production through an intelligible substance. This removes a particular explanatory requirement. It does not independently establish the warrant of every attribution made using that comparison.

The external-world sequence is especially damaging to the earlier HPA bridge. T1.4.2.20 describes a porter, a door and a letter: reconciling the evidence appears to require a continuous surrounding world. But .21 immediately denies that ordinary causal custom by itself supplies the extra continuity. Observed interrupted regularity cannot directly establish a more complete unobserved regularity. Paragraph .22 adds imagination’s tendency to continue its course. The explanation thus contains an operation beyond the experienced conjunction that our earlier proposal treated as sufficient. Later in the sequence, .47 denies experience of the conjunction between perceptions and independent archetypes; .54 separately challenges resemblance. The concluding recovery in .57 proceeds under renewed everyday supposition. This is an argument against the stated grounding route, not an argument that no bodies exist. [T1.4.2](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/2).

The passage cannot be repaired merely by adding “familiar kind.” The target in question is the uninterrupted existence of the objects behind perceptual interruptions; the available experiential regularity does not already contain that target. Nor does explanatory indispensability in .20 settle the point, because Hume himself subjects its inferential source to the subsequent criticism. An explanation of why we adopt a completion must not silently become evidence that the completion is true.

Composition introduces a second dependency. T1.2.2–3 combines finite perceptual capacity, minimal units and represented extension. The question is whether actual organized constituents imply an additional single complex being. Hakkarainen’s restricted distinction and Baxter’s collective interpretation answer that question differently. T1.4.3 explains the invented bearer of connected qualities, while T1.4.5 distinguishes spatially arranged perceptions from passions and other nonextended perceptions. A spatial solution therefore cannot simply be transferred to every mental union. [T1.2.2](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/2/2), [T1.2.3](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/2/3), [T1.4.3](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/3), [T1.4.5](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/5).

T1.4.6 offers a related succession and an easy transition of thought in place of strict invariant identity. Its republic analogy allows a person’s character to change. The stronger reconstruction would derive one consciousness from the causal pattern. But the argument already concerns a train assigned to a person. Assignment and causal connectedness are different premises. Sympathy provides a discriminating case: T2.1.11 describes causal communication between people, and .16–17 distinguishes relations converging on the same people from relations distributed across different people. If every causal connection constitutes one mind, communicating people collapse into one. If invariant character supplies ownership, the permitted character change defeats its necessity. The bare-graph countermodel holds only causal edges fixed. Hume’s actual evidence also includes bodies, memories, kinship, proximity and context; it does not show that those richer data leave ordinary ownership undetermined. [T1.4.6](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/6), [T2.1.11](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/2/1/11).

This dependency has a practical consequence for the HPA account of other minds. Inferring pain from a kind of sign differs from assigning that episode to a continuing personal history. The latter changes character estimates and moral judgments. T3.3.1.4–7 treats durable character as evidence in judgments about actions already attributed to people; it does not first derive personal ownership from that character. Tracking bodies, memory and context may jointly support assignment, and Hume’s person-indexed descriptions already employ such evidence. We identify a dependency of the explanation, not a demonstrated defect in every actual person-specific inference. Current episode attribution must also be separated from a complete constitution of one continuing conscious history. A bounded diagnostic case clarifies the targets: a presently recognized patient’s bodily signs may support a present pain attribution even if no evidence yet connects that patient to yesterday’s episode. This is a derived limiting case, not a newly discovered Hume example. The ordinary signs-and-sympathy argument meets the known-relation condition through repeated associations and a recognized patient; extension to a continuing history adds memory, bodily trajectory or contextual identification. Neither present attribution nor that extension alone supplies strict invariant identity or one consciousness.

It is circular to present the same stable-character generalization as both the sole basis of assignment and its subsequent confirmation. This does not require an immaterial bearer; it requires exposing the premise actually used. [T3.3.1](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/3/3/1).

## The Appendix withdraws confidence in explanatory completion

The 1740 Appendix explicitly contrasts the earlier hope expressed at T1.4.5.1 with a present failure to explain personal identity. Appendix20–21 retains distinct perceptions and lack of a perceived real connection while admitting difficulty explaining their union in consciousness. It supplies no completed replacement. The specific difficulty may concern reflected connection or association rather than ownership boundaries alone; our ownership diagnosis does not exhaust Hume’s admission. The earlier claim of explanatory completion must therefore be qualified; it does not follow that every constituent-level causal account is withdrawn. Appendix2–9 simultaneously retains and clarifies causal belief, including an acquaintance inferred from voice and partially visible body in .4. Local explanation and unresolved constitution coexist. [Appendix](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/app).

This is a genuine developmental finding because Hume marks the failed explanatory ambition himself. Its further structural consequence is ours: the retained causal-belief account is not presented as a satisfactory explanation of the union of successive perceptions in thought or consciousness. Reading the admitted failure specifically as episode ownership remains a proposed diagnosis against other interpretations. Book 3 appeared with the Appendix in 1740, but publication together does not establish their composition order or prove that its character-based judgments were a deliberate repair.

The Abstract supplies a particularly strong control: .27 puts continued-existence belief and causal belief in the same kind of sentiment, while .13–16/.25 retain custom and unproved uniformity, and .33 relates testimony and history to causal evidence. The objection is that our separation artificially divides what Hume joins. The response is that shared phenomenology does not establish an identical generating operation; T1.4.2.21–22 expressly adds the further imagination operation. This is a distinction within a belief explanation, not a claim that body belief lacks vivacity. The public Abstract witness uses the March1740 edition with handwritten corrections; the corrections are not all independently dated March1740. [Abstract](https://davidhume.org/texts/a).

The edited web text integrates Appendix interventions. In particular, the opening replacement at T1.2.4.22 and insertion at .31 must not be silently assigned to 1739. Our full section readings used that edited witness; the chronological record separates these marked interventions. First-edition facsimile collation remains unfinished. No inference about an exact original wording is made where that distinction matters.

## Limits that depend on the conclusion: testimony and religious inference

Ordinary testimony and animal reasoning are counterexamples to a blanket refusal of absent mental activity. They also do not prove perception-independent persons. The own-case analogy has an existing direct precedent in Kim; Inoue considers that proposal while defending a different person-perception account. Neither is a new HPA discovery. Their disagreement concerns how the evidential relation works, not just whether mental vocabulary occurs.

EHU11’s represented friend distinguishes familiar human artificers from a cause characterized only through the observed universe. If the cause is estimated entirely from its effect, additional attributes or future consequences do not acquire support simply because we have given the cause a name. Relevant independent experience can supply the missing premise for a familiar human kind. Within the friend’s conceded argument, this limit concerns the enriched conclusion. The narrator’s final objection at EHU11.30 also questions the initial inference to a wholly singular cause outside experienced kinds. Our reconstruction must preserve that first-inference challenge. Cleanthes’ voice is diagnostic through intelligible speech; its uniqueness does not make world order equally diagnostic. Cleanthes’ extraordinary articulate voice in Dialogues III tests the categorical ban; Philo’s limited analogy in XII tests the opposite enlargement. Speaker and concession remain part of the reconstruction: a dialogue speaker’s assertion is not automatically Hume’s unqualified doctrine. [EHU11](https://davidhume.org/texts/e/11), [Dialogues III](https://davidhume.org/texts/d/3), [Dialogues XII](https://davidhume.org/texts/d/12).

EHU10 gives a sharper adverse case for an overgenerous calibration reading. Its imagined eight-day darkness permits comparative testimony for an extraordinary occurrence. The religious-miracle policy in .38 is nevertheless severe. A reconstruction must retain the stated background of religious enthusiasm, deception and testimonial failure; it cannot pretend the same passage simply treats every report neutrally. Our claim is that this policy requires those evidential premises, not that Hume never issues a categorical verdict once he adopts them. Novelty alone is not the operative defect in every case. [EHU10](https://davidhume.org/texts/e/10).

The Natural History’s Introduction is strong counterevidence to universal abstention: it directly affirms a rational intelligent-author inference while separately investigating religion’s human origin. It cannot be discarded as irony without an argument. The Moral Enquiry’s Appendix1.21 likewise presents reason as discovering how objects stand in nature. The preceding discussion begins conditionally at .1 and examines a contrary hypothesis at .4; that qualification does not erase .21’s affirmative contrast between reason and taste. These passages resist a uniform translation of every positive claim into merely convenient fiction. The NHR affirmation is stronger than Philo’s conditional thin analogy; the tension cannot be erased by treating their voices and strengths as identical. Whether their affirmation is ultimately active endorsement remains a further interpretive question. [NHR Introduction](https://davidhume.org/texts/n/0), [Moral Enquiry Appendix1](https://davidhume.org/texts/m/app1).

## Historical evidence supplies a positive test, not an ornamental parallel

The opening of “Of the Balance of Power” makes the target dependence unusually explicit. Even if Xenophon’s narrative were entirely fictional, its representation of political balancing establishes an ancient available notion. The reasoning conjoins a surviving authored representation with its conceptual content and the author’s historical situation. It does not infer that the represented alliance occurred. A false narrative can therefore remain evidence for one proposition while failing as evidence for another. The same paragraph does not license all narratives: attribution, dating and semantic interpretation remain necessary premises. Clarendon 2021, volume1, pp.252–257; variants pp.634–635.

This control improves the inference model. Causal plausibility cannot be sufficient for narrated-event truth because a romance can reproduce plausible motives. Yet wholesale rejection of the narrative throws away its evidence about representable concepts. Neither “successful explanation establishes its objects” nor “fiction is epistemically useless” captures Hume’s target distinction. The extension from philosophy to this essay is warranted by an explicit alternative inference in the text, not by matching terms such as evidence or cause.

The deleted early Roman-history note adds a developmental control. It supports comparison of opposed accounts and politically proportioned revolutions while criticizing exaggerated military numbers. The critical edition identifies it only in the two 1752 states, 52a and 52b; it is absent from 1754 onward. Its omission removes an explicit methodological defence at that location. It does not erase the essay’s other Greek and Roman evidence or prove abandonment of causal comparison. The retained opening inference from fictional representation supplies an independent argumentative route for the limited conceptual conclusion. We do not claim that this independent route was newly invented at deletion.

History1.1–5 supplies another contextual test. The opening rejects unreliable remote-origin traditions, uses comparative cultural traces, and explains why travellers magnify accounts. Hume’s practice distinguishes target questions and source-generating motives. It also contains contemporary judgments about peoples that we do not certify as true. A historical application illustrates his evidential practice; it cannot by itself settle the authority of the psychological theory explaining that practice. [History1](https://davidhume.org/texts/h/1).

The publicly accessible extract of Hume’s letter to Gilbert Elliot, 18 February1751, contrasts fact-constrained political/natural-philosophical reasoning with metaphysical and theological disagreement lacking comparably plain standards. This is corroboration from a private statement, not a substitute for the published argument or evidence of what every 1739 premise meant. Only the extract was read. [Elliot extract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/hume-dialogues-concerning-natural-religion/letters-to-gilbert-elliot-extracts/5497F5A60ECE81E80BFFEDEF5C40C420).

Pedro Faria’s 2021 study already investigates Hume’s historical evidence, causal plausibility and critical source comparison. We therefore claim no discovery that historical writing matters for his epistemology. Our narrower use of the Xenophon alternative is a target-specific dependency test. A complete priority search for that diagnosis has not been performed. [Faria](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1479244319000404).

## Assent: the strongest objection to our account

T1.4.1’s fallibility regress and T1.4.7’s recovery cannot be solved by observing that Hume continues reasoning. Baxter’s original Pyrrhonian account allows strong, articulate, corrective passive assent. Its important evidence is the regress’s normative force and the concluding dilemma, not the claim that a sceptic must become mute. Custom’s involuntariness also does not logically imply the impossibility of endorsement. That would add a disputed bridge of its own.

Garrett’s reply is correspondingly stronger than a demand that inquiry needs beliefs. First-order evidence may provide additional probable evidence for a faculty’s reliability; its semantic ascent opposes sceptical assessments without directly rebutting every sceptical argument. Garrett grants the possibility of global error and denies an a priori balancing rule. His constitutive standard of probability supplies the normative component. Moreover, his reply at pp.293–296 expressly treats senses and memory as noninferential sources of epistemically reputable factual belief: a composite sensory faculty may remain acceptable despite an identity-misattributing sub-operation. He need not defend the causal-only route that HPA proposed. Lack of a faculty-independent foundation therefore cannot alone refute him. [T1.4.1](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/1), [T1.4.7](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/7); original-source reconstructions and page references are in [the development assessment](../notes/development-v5.md).

The strongest objection is that our two levels manufacture a difficulty Garrett has already dissolved: if ordinary evidence constitutes the standard, a demand for an independent frame is misplaced. Our response is restricted but substantive. Garrett may defend normative evidence in that way; the porter sequence still denies that the specific causal-custom mechanism establishes extra continuity, and personal grouping still needs information absent from bare causal connectedness. A constitutive standard does not repair the causal-only derivation, but Garrett supplies a different, noninferential route. His composite-faculty defence must explain how general body content remains acceptable after naïve perceptual identity is defeated; that is a pressure requiring assessment, not a failure established here. Conversely, these content failures do not prove Baxter’s global conclusion. The corrected account identifies different burdens rather than declaring victory over either rival.

EHU12 retains the external-world problem alongside corrigibility and a narrowed inquiry. The edited witness is 1777; we have not collated all corresponding 1748 wording. T1.4.7.14 already recommends examination, limits and scepticism about sceptical doubts, so a developmental story in which the Enquiry first invents durable moderation fails. Qu’s 2025 original explicitly treats increased actionable guidance and audience; that established proposal is neither our novelty nor proof that the later work supplies ultimate vindication. EHU12.3’s criticism of antecedent universal doubt does not by itself withdraw a regress consequent on experience of fallibility. [EHU12](https://davidhume.org/texts/e/12), [Qu2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/can.2025.8).

## Formal results and their textual limits

The [argument model](../models/integrated-v5.json) records conjunctive edges and distinguishes textual assertions from required or HPA-added bridges. The [checker](../scripts/check_reconstruction_v5.py) tests translations, not Hume’s endorsement of our axioms.

Some countercases are explicit stipulated truth assignments rather than rich-world searches. The checker validates identifiers and premise references; it does not validate chronological metadata or historical fidelity. First, ignorance of a bearer’s nature is compatible both with existence and with nonexistence. It therefore entails neither existence nor suspension without a further epistemic premise. Second, a causal graph admits different assignments of episodes to personal histories; a connected component does not determine a consciousness. Third, a dependent real whole and unrestricted independent separability of every numerically distinct existent conflict when the whole and its essential part are both in the domain. Hakkarainen restricts the separation principle; Baxter supplies collective existence without an additional strictly existing whole. Their repairs differ, and neither is refuted by our logical alternative.

Fourth, Appendix21’s two stated principles alone are satisfiable. “No perceived real connection” is not “no actual connection.” The checker’s exact minimal conflict adds a unity demand, denial of a simple bearer and an exhaustive bridge identifying possible grounds to the no-perceived-connection premise. Distinctness is not part of that minimal conflicting set. The Appendix presents an explanatory failure; it does not prove that its two sentences are a minimal logical contradiction. Fifth, positive assertion, corrective practice and even strong belief do not by themselves entail active endorsement. A formal countermodel establishes the need for an additional premise, not that passive assent is historically correct.

## What changed, what remains open, and coverage

The previous claim that explanatory independence from substance supports evidential independence has been withdrawn in its strong form. The revised account makes an actual difference: it preserves some absent-episode and conceptual-history inferences while blocking the enlarged conclusions about uninterrupted objects, unified consciousness and further divine attributes unless additional premises are supplied. It explains why these limits occur: evidence suitable for one target does not automatically contain the constitution, ownership or extra attributes demanded by another.

Hakkarainen and Baxter remain genuine rivals over real composition; Garrett and Baxter remain rivals over reflective authority. Our result does not reconcile those ontologies or decide that all assent is passive. It exposes a questionable premise connecting those disputes: that a verdict about composition or an explanation without substance already determines what empirical attribution warrants. The personal-history dependency is a candidate consequential intervention. Its originality and broader fidelity need comparison with the full personal-identity and other-minds literature; no scholarly advance is certified here.

The corpus audit searched all21 preserved TEI members, including six History volumes and three correspondence collections, by a specified small term set. The earlier index covered11 members. Searchability is not reading or comprehension. Complete contextual readings for active problems, selected supplementary readings, and gaps are separately recorded in [coverage](../models/coverage-v5.json) and the three assessments. History reading was limited to1.1–5 and the letter to a public extract. The Moral Enquiry Appendix and NHR Introduction were complete units; the dissertations’ broader arguments and all correspondence were not reconstructed. No all-Hume model is claimed.

Complete accessible originals of Hakkarainen2012/2015 and Baxter’s substance/assent arguments support the central comparisons. Garrett’s accessible dialogue and reply arguments were directly assessed, but his 1997 book and complete2007 article remain access gaps. Hakkarainen2019 likewise remains unassessed after legitimate route retries. Qu2025 was read completely; Faria was read selectively. Kim’s complete original argument was read in this cycle; the selected root references do not represent a whole other-minds literature review. Earlier dossier claims remain provisional, with no full reassessment of every dispute disguised as a new result.

The experiment shows a useful capacity and a material failure: the system can expose a bridge, derive adverse consequences and replace an overextended reconstruction, but initially overread compatible evidence as grounding. Shared-model opposition is not independent historical evidence. Exact deployment settings were unavailable; prior exposure was substantial; no untouched holdout or human evaluation occurred. A philosopher should assess the specific dependency diagnosis and the surviving rival arguments, rather than infer success from the number of citations or formal checks.
