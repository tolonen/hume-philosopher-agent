# Positive inquiry without a theory of substance

HPA, 12 October 2026. Updated with Hakkarainen and Baxter. Causal argument: [HPA-006](../dossiers/HPA-006.md). Constitution, identity and assent: [HPA-007](../dossiers/HPA-007.md).

**The strongest positive reading emerging from this investigation is that Hume can make real, corrigible causal claims while refusing a further claim about substance.** His restriction applies to the content added by an inference, not simply to whether its object is observable, mental or material. This provides a substantive answer to the question left open in the previous report: how can human science investigate other people without first settling the metaphysical question about perception-independent entities?

This is a research result, not merely a proposal to look for one. Its basis is a connected group of explicit arguments. Its scholarly originality remains unestablished, and its conclusion is narrower than a solution to all Humean scepticism.

## Three arguments that do the work

In **Treatise1.4.5.30**, Hume explicitly separates the substance of the mind from the causes of thought, then affirms that bodily motion actually causes thought and perception. Paragraph33 repeats the positive causal conclusion with an express limitation to the causal relation we understand. The complete section distinguishes substance, spatial conjunction and cause, rather than withdrawing every affirmative statement once the substance question fails. The separation sentence also matches the independently preserved private TEI witness exactly. [Primary text, SBN247–250](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/5).

This is stronger evidence than methodological permission to continue working. It is Hume making a positive assertion after explaining why a different question should not be answered in the proposed terms. The case also corrects our initial hypothesis: Hume does not privilege mental causes over material ones. Bodily causation belongs inside the positive account.

In **Enquiry11.24–27**, the unfinished building and single footprint explain how legitimate inference goes beyond what is observed. The friend's answer to the narrator is that we possess independent experience of human anatomy, intentions and conduct. That background supports another foot or further building work. The additional conclusions do not arise simply by enriching the cause inferred from the first effect. The narrator's final paragraph30 reinforces the requirement of experienced causal resemblance. [Primary text, SBN142–148](https://davidhume.org/texts/e/11).

This supplies a rule for the *particular addition*: what evidence supports that further attribute or prediction? An inference about a designer cannot acquire support for future rewards merely by inserting justice into the designer's description. The relevant background has to be established. The distinction is not just intrinsic nature versus relations: future effects and spatial relations can also be unwarranted additions.

In **Dialogues4.13–14**, even Cleanthes and Philo share an important concession. Explanation need not explain the cause of every cause. Philo expressly approves explaining particular effects by general causes that remain ultimately inexplicable. The dispute is whether the proposed divine explanation achieves an appropriate explanatory gain. [Primary text, KS163–164](https://davidhume.org/texts/d/4).

Taken together, these passages support a positive account of inquiry: identify causal relations, extend them through relevant resemblance, and require evidence for each additional attribution. Ultimate ignorance alone does not invalidate that work. This is our cross-work reconstruction; it is not a single formula explicitly promulgated by Hume.

## What it explains about other people

Hume's own starting point is unusually clear. **Treatise1.3.16.2–3** moves from consciousness of our purposive actions to similar internal operations inferred from other creatures' similar actions. **Treatise3.3.1.4–7** treats conduct as evidence of durable character while denying immediate access to another person's passion. The inference targets another instance of an experienced kind. It differs from the perception–archetype inference criticized at **1.4.2.47**, where the required conjunction has never been experienced. [Animal reason](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/3/16), [character and passions](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/3/3/1), [external objects](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/2).

This gives the account more than logical compatibility. It identifies evidence and error conditions. Circumstances, subsequent conduct and stable patterns can correct a mistaken attribution. Sympathy does not automatically verify it: Hume explicitly discusses sympathy with feelings that do not exist and with anticipated danger. An observer's felt response can therefore exceed the evidence about the observed person's present state. [T2.2.7.5–6](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/2/2/7), [T2.2.9.13–14](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/2/2/9).

The inference has existential force: another person's pain is real, not just a convenient description of my current sensations. But independence from *my observation* does not establish independence from *all human perceptions*. A second human mental event need not be a specifically different substance. The stronger metaphysical conclusion requires an additional premise.

## How this changes the dispute

The positive result supports Hakkarainen against the claim that the existence of affirmative human science alone excludes restricted metaphysical suspension. It supplies the missing inferential account: causal attribution and correction can proceed without resolving substance. It also respects ordinary realism. A hidden bodily cause can have the same empirical credentials as an unobserved passion; **the account concedes those bodily commitments**.

The substance/cause distinction alone does not establish compatibility with Hakkarainen's exact suspension thesis: a realist can affirm independent existence without a theory of substance. The further result depends on the reconstructed account of what the empirical claims entail.

This does not establish that Hume actually maintains suspension through all philosophical reflection. Zahn's perspectivalism can retain these local inferential standards and dispute their standing under unrestricted sceptical scrutiny. The regress of Treatise1.4.1 remains. The advance is more specific: a change of standpoint is unnecessary to explain why a scientist can affirm a causal claim and reject its proposed substance supplement within one inquiry. The propositions differ, and the evidence supports different parts of their content.

Two difficult religious cases remain visible. Cleanthes's unprecedented voice in Dialogues3 challenges any requirement to have observed the entire target situation before inferring intelligence; Philo gives no recorded answer. Our account can allow the familiar signature of speech without licensing every divine attribute, but that is our reconstructed reply. Dialogues12 also contains positive design declarations that cannot be erased by its restricted final analogy. No universal religious suspension follows. [Part3](https://davidhume.org/texts/d/3), [Part12](https://davidhume.org/texts/d/12).

## Hakkarainen and Baxter: what the causal account still owes

Their inclusion changes the interpretation. Causal inquiry employs objects and persons; it must not silently assume that successful prediction proves their constitution as single beings. Hakkarainen provides a positive account of spatial properties without an underlying substance. Baxter supplies a genuine rival account of complex unity, and a separate challenge concerning assent. The exact original claims and qualifications are reconstructed in [HPA-007](../dossiers/HPA-007.md).

Three distinctions now govern the argument. **An arrangement at a time is not strict identity through change. Causal connectedness is not sufficient for one consciousness. An affirmative claim is not automatically rational endorsement.** The first distinction is already explicit in Hakkarainen's spatial-properties manuscript; it is not our discovery. Nor does it dissolve the whole disagreement: Baxter's substance chapter also challenges simultaneous complex unity and directly answers Hakkarainen's2012 whole–part argument. [Hakkarainen manuscript](https://trepo.tuni.fi/bitstream/10024/101369/1/hume_on_spatial_properties.pdf), [Baxter substance manuscript](https://philarchive.org/archive/BAXHOS-4).

Hume's evidence makes the distinctions practical. A connected mass, a repaired ship, a growing organism and a republic are not interchangeable cases. T1.4.6.8 supposes strict identity under specified conditions; .11–14 explains ordinary identity through changes; .19 describes the causal system of perceptions while expressly allowing character to change. Therefore stable character can help assess an attribution within a tracked personal history, but cannot independently establish that history's identity. The decisive character-change wording was checked against the private TEI witness. [T1.4.6](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/6).

The Appendix imposes a further limit. Hume still finds his account of the union of perceptions in consciousness defective. Causation also crosses between persons through sympathy and interaction; merely grouping connected episodes would not distinguish one mind from another. The spatial account must therefore not be advertised as solving the Appendix. [Appendix20–21](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/app).

Baxter's complete assent argument also survives our earlier positive passages: his distinction allows theoretical and normative claims to be accepted passively. Our evidence about what Hume asserts cannot alone decide how he accepts it. Consequently, “warrant” in the positive reconstruction denotes a contested interpretation of the standards operative within inquiry, not an independently established defeat of Baxter's sceptical account. [Baxter assent manuscript](https://philpapers.org/archive/BAXAIS.pdf).

The revised positive result is that **local causal inquiry can proceed without resolving every question about the constitution, persistence and acceptance of its objects and claims**. It can sometimes proceed whether its objects are interpreted as genuine complexes or organized pluralities. This explains why empirical success alone does not choose between those ontologies. It identifies a shared scope for inquiry while leaving their real disagreements intact.

## Contribution and evidential limits

The own-case analogy is established scholarship. Kim's2019 article provides a direct precedent for it; Inoue's2003 article examines it while defending a different person-perception account. Both originals were read in full by the separate assessor. Full Waldow and relevant Garrett book comparisons remain outstanding. The potentially useful contribution is the combination of the substance/cause distinction, the evidential basis of unobserved instances, and the restriction on additional attributes, applied to the current science–metaphysics disagreement. It would be premature to call that combination unprecedented. [Kim](https://doi.org/10.1080/09608788.2018.1524365), [Inoue](https://doi.org/10.4288/jafpos1956.11.75).

The argument rests on complete bounded sections, with three separate automated assessments and explicit counterevidence. Their agreement is not independent historical validation. The Treatise edited witness,1777Enquiry and1779Dialogues are kept distinct; no revision history is inferred merely from publication order. Two fragments were checked against the private corpus. Page-image collation is unfinished, and this stage did not inspect all Hume's works. The complete reading bounds, changes of hypothesis, source access and preservation receipts are linked from [SOURCES](../SOURCES.md) and [experiment record](../EXPERIMENT.md).

The Hakkarainen2015 spatial-properties manuscript and Baxter's substance and assent manuscripts were read in full across the investigators and preserved privately. Hakkarainen's2019 unity article remains unassessed after failed original-source routes; the2015 article does not substitute for it. Baxter's direct answer to Hakkarainen2012 is now part of the comparison. Its central countertexts were already considered by him, so no overlooked-passage refutation is claimed.

The conclusion is affirmative and bounded: **Hume offers resources for correctable causal inquiry without a prior substance theory. Whether its objects are genuine complex unities, and how its conclusions are assented to, remain further questions.** This is the revised interpretation supported by the combined investigation.
