# What this experiment attempted, did and found

**Question:** can an LLM, working with primary texts and original scholarship, produce a substantial contribution to history of philosophy, rather than plausible summaries or speculative criticism? This experiment uses Hume to make that question concrete. It is a single interactive research episode with a documented argument record. Its intellectual quality awaits external assessment.

The work took place across four substantive stages on 12 October 2026. Mikko Tolonen set the research question and standards, supplied access to existing research resources, and redirected the investigation. A Codex LLM assistant selected claims, retrieved texts, reconstructed arguments, wrote dossiers and integrated separate automated assessments. No philosopher has yet validated the conclusions. The scholars discussed were not contacted.

## 1. Aim and success standard

The original objective was ambitious: identify consequential errors in published Hume interpretations, demonstrate them from Hume's works, survive the scholar's strongest reasonable response, and offer a better reading. It expressly prohibited manufacturing refutations. Vindicating a targeted argument or showing underdetermination was a legitimate outcome. A scholarly intervention required an additional novelty check and a clear account of its consequence.

The brief covered eight areas: causation, induction, representation, belief, scepticism, identity, motivation and liberty. Actual investigation was narrower. The first stage examined causal semantics and motivational argument; later stages concentrated on induction, restricted scepticism, separability, constitution, other minds and assent. An eight-area register is an agenda, not eight areas of completed research.

After the first delivery, the user asked for deeper debates around Garrett and Hakkarainen, metaphysics proper and substantial support that might discriminate rival positions. A further direction called for positive reconstruction with more Humean evidence. Baxter was then explicitly added. These interventions matter for evaluation: the model did not independently choose the entire research trajectory or satisfy the positive-reading demand on its first attempt.

## 2. Procedure actually used

The project reused an existing privately held primary-text archive. A searchable witness was created for eleven work/correspondence members, including the Treatise and Appendix, Abstract, Enquiries, essays and Dialogues. The original archive has twenty-one members; History volumes were not included in that index. Index integrity and selected literal fragments were checked. These are retrieval and provenance checks, not proof that the entire corpus was read or that every paragraph reference was collated.

Public Hume Texts Online transcriptions supplied independently accessible passage wording and surrounding sections. The edited Treatise incorporates interventions, including Appendix material. The Enquiry witness follows the 1777 edition; the Dialogues witness follows 1779. Later texts were kept distinct from the earlier Treatise, and speakers in dialogues were identified. Stable section references and SBN/KS labels were recorded; labels are not independently inspected page images.

Original scholarship was read through accessible journal texts, author manuscripts and publisher HTML. The ledgers distinguish complete accessible-text inspection, selective inspection, indirect evidence and unassessed originals. Complete reading means that the investigator inspected the accessible text, notes and references; it does not guarantee perfect comprehension, final-version equivalence or facsimile collation. An author's reply was not treated as a substitute for reading an opponent's entire argument.

For each investigated claim the assistant attempted a chain: **target → primary evidence → labelled interpretive premises → inference → precise verdict**. It searched for qualifications, replies and contrary passages. Separate automated agents were assigned to defend scholars, examine primary evidence or challenge the positive reconstruction. No controlled blinded assessment was used; the agents shared the research context. Their checks may expose a weak step; their agreement is not independent evidence about Hume.

A small finite script checked two schematic claims about implication and overlapping wholes. It proves consequences within stipulated models. It does not prove that Hume or any scholar accepts those models, or select the correct interpretation of separability. No model training was undertaken. Full source payloads were kept outside GitHub, and no complete scholarly articles or licensed corpus files are supplied here.

## 3. Results of the first two investigations

### Millican: an attempted refutation that did not survive

HPA-001 targeted Millican's 2009 claim that the role of Hume's definitions in liberty and necessity requires a semantic interpretation. The attempted objection distinguished uniform warranted causal attribution from exhaustive reduction of the causal referent. T1.4.5.33's qualification about the causal relation we understand, unknown qualities in T1.3.14.27, and the religious concession in T2.3.2.4 appeared to allow a weaker reconstruction.

The defence restored the difficult evidence: T1.3.14.33 denies a foundation in nature for two kinds of necessity, T2.3.1.4 identifies essential particulars, and Abstract34 demands another idea while denying its availability. An alternative must explain these assertions, not just establish abstract compatibility. Millican had already distinguished pragmatic sufficiency from semantic determination. His 2011 discussion conditionally allows a successful extended-reference account, while questioning its textual basis. Beebee's 2007 article supplies the relevant published precedent.

**Outcome:** withdraw the proposed consequential refutation. A contestable alternative and a familiar distinction were reproduced; neither is a discovery. This is a useful test of correction, but not successful novel criticism. See [HPA-001](dossiers/HPA-001.md).

### Zimmerman: argumentative function and argumentative adequacy

HPA-002 initially questioned whether the representation argument establishes reason's causal impotence, rather than a distinction between passions and truth-apt representations. Hume's own retrospective discussion in T3.1.1.8–10 supplies the motivational function, and Zimmerman already cites and qualifies that evidence. The first objection therefore attacked an artificially exclusive division.

An elementary countermodel shows that some nonmotivating reasoning does not entail the impossibility of reasoning motivating. But Zimmerman's stronger reconstruction includes a function-delimiting premise and surrounding psychology. Refuting a weaker implication does not refute his complete argument.

**Outcome:** withdraw the initial objection; retain an unpromoted question about the taxonomy and adequacy of the functional reconstruction. No refutation of the wider account of non-instrumental reasons follows. See [HPA-002](dossiers/HPA-002.md).

## 4. More discriminating debates

### Garrett and Millican on induction

The complete 2011 dialogue and Garrett's later reply complicated easy objections. Garrett permits bad probable reasoning and does not prohibit reflective reasoning from contributing to inference. T1.3.8.14 is especially useful: Hume reinstates custom indirectly in the reflective single-experiment case. T1.3.6 and T1.4.4 support separating a process's dependence on custom from whether it is epistemically good or bad.

**Outcome:** qualified support for Garrett's customary-dependence account. The classification of a persuasive but failed demonstration remains a genuine pressure point; the evidence does not decide his entire epistemology. See [HPA-004](dossiers/HPA-004.md).

### Hakkarainen, Zahn and positive science

T1.3.5.2 allows causal explanation from coherent perceptions while leaving their ultimate origin undetermined. That challenges the claim that constructive science, simply by existing, rules out restricted metaphysical suspension. Hakkarainen's realism target concerns objects independent of human perception, not merely experiences beyond my present observation. Zahn's position includes first-order science and must not be caricatured as making every ordinary judgement false.

Book 2 creates the stronger problem: social inference involves other people's affections and real existence. Explanatory independence from physiology is not evidential independence from other persons. A suspension account must explain these commitments; its opponent must establish the relevant kind of independence and assent. Demeter provides a published methodological precedent, not a newly discovered agreement among all the authors.

**Outcome:** positive science alone does not decide the issue. Methodological permission does not prove warrant or actual suspension. See [HPA-003](dossiers/HPA-003.md).

### Hakkarainen, Clay, Qu and separability

T1.2.3.13 permits separating a point from the other points. It does not say that the identical whole survives removal of that point. This supports Hakkarainen's local whole–part repair. But extending the repair into an exclusive general restriction needs an additional argument.

Overlapping complexes show how nonoverlap and noninclusion restrictions differ: W(a,b) and V(b,c) can each exist without the other in suitable cases while sharing b. This was already published by Qu and discussed by Clay; it is not a model discovery. Baxter's direct reply further challenges whether a whole is another strictly existing thing at all.

**Outcome:** limited defence of the local repair; no adjudication of the entire ontology. See [HPA-005](dossiers/HPA-005.md) and [HPA-007](dossiers/HPA-007.md).

## 5. The positive reconstruction and its argument

The most substantial candidate is a combined account of local causal inquiry, developed in HPA-006 and narrowed by HPA-007. Its premises and limits are:

1. **Textual separation:** T1.4.5.30 separates the mind's substance from the cause of thought, then affirms bodily motion as a cause. T1.4.5.33 retains the causal conclusion. A positive causal assertion therefore need not answer the substance question. This also defeats our early proposal to privilege mental causes while excluding material causes.
2. **Independent analogical background:** EHU11.24–30 distinguishes inferring a familiar human cause, supported by other experience of that kind, from inferring an unfamiliar cause from one effect and then freely enriching it with attributes and predictions. The friend supplies much of the discussion; the narrator's final formulation supports the relevant similarity requirement. This is an evidential restriction, not permission to ignore the work's dialogue structure.
3. **Other-mind attribution:** T1.3.16.2–3 and Book 2's signs, belief and sympathy furnish resources for inference from familiar actions and internal operations to other creatures' states. Sympathy alone is not verification: Hume also describes feelings attributed where no corresponding feeling exists. Kim supplies a direct precedent for the own-case analogy; Inoue examines it while defending a different person-perception account.
4. **The proposed interpretive bridge:** conduct, context and independent familiarity can constrain particular causal attributions without establishing an additional nonperceptual bearer. This is the contested reconstruction. An existential claim about another feeling does not alone entail Hakkarainen's stronger perception-independent ontology. Equally, ignorance of substance does not prove suspension about external existence.
5. **Constitution remains open:** Hakkarainen offers real spatial complexes without underlying substance; Baxter interprets apparent wholes through parts collectively related. Some predictions can operate on either account. Predictive success therefore cannot by itself choose which unit of existence Hume admits.
6. **Identity remains open:** T1.4.6 distinguishes strict identity from ordinary continuity in masses, ships, organisms and persons. Paragraph19 expressly allows character to change. Stable character can help assess an attribution within a tracked history; it cannot establish the person's identity. Causal links also cross between people, so connectedness alone does not individuate one consciousness. The Appendix reports an unresolved defect.
7. **Assent remains open:** Baxter's account permits affirmative theoretical and normative content without active endorsement. Positive scientific language therefore does not alone defeat his Pyrrhonian reading. The reconstruction's empirical standards do not settle their ultimate epistemic authority or answer the higher-order sceptical regress.

**Precise conclusion:** Hume offers resources for correctable local causal inquiry without a prior substratum theory. Whether the objects of that inquiry are genuine complex unities, how they persist, and how its conclusions are accepted require further arguments. This does not establish global anti-sceptical justification, metaphysical suspension, composition realism, nihilism, or a solution of the Appendix.

The possible scholarly interest is the combination of these distinctions and passages as a constraint on the science–metaphysics debate. Its novelty and explanatory superiority remain unestablished. A philosopher may reasonably judge it to be useful synthesis, a partial advance, an overgeneralization, or a largely familiar result.

## 6. What the experiment did not achieve

The original ambition of a consequential, novel refutation was not met. Adding evidence narrowed several claims rather than producing a decisive victory. No important passage is advertised as newly discovered; the known unity/identity distinction is in Hakkarainen, familiar countertexts are already considered by Baxter, and own-case analogy has precedents. A failure to find further precedent is not proof of originality.

Material unread sources include Hakkarainen's 2019 unity article, full Qu2022, relevant complete Garrett book treatments, Waldow and Baxter's complete book. Access problems and a legacy Word format issue are recorded separately from conclusions about the arguments. Accessible 2015 manuscripts cannot stand in for unavailable later work. Manuscript and published pagination sometimes differ, and decisive final-version/facsimile collation is unfinished.

The experiment lacks a pre-registered selection rule, untouched evaluation set, human baseline, blind expert review and repeated runs. Prior training exposure is unknown. Research-stage records leave exact deployment and sampling settings unspecified. Thus it supports inspection of this workflow and its outputs, not an accuracy rate, a causal estimate of LLM benefit or a general claim that LLMs advance philosophy.

The public edition contains selected prompts and retrospective role summaries, current dossiers, earlier stage reports and reading ledgers. It starts a fresh Git history and omits private operational receipts, source payloads, private locators and full execution records. A separate user-requested chat transcript exists outside this public edition. The revision log preserves important changes, but the public package is not an exact replay environment.

## 7. What an external evaluation can decide

The [review guide](REVIEW_GUIDE.md) asks a philosopher to identify the strongest and weakest specific claims, inspect original contexts, test the interpretive bridges, assess the replies, locate precedents, and judge whether anything exceeds competent synthesis. A negative result is a legitimate evaluation. Finding that the model corrected itself would still be different from finding that it made a new contribution to Hume scholarship.
