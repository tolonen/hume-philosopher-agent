# HPA-003: Does Hume's positive science require metaphysical realism?

Date: 12 October 2026. Completed bounded argument cycle. **Verdict: a contestable alternative strengthened; the appeal to positive science alone does not exclude restricted suspension.** No refutation of Zahn's complete interpretation, proof of Hakkarainen's complete interpretation, or originality claim.

## Exact target and source status

Sam Zahn, *Is Hume a Perspectivalist?*, *Ergo* 12(1) (2025), 1–34, [original](https://journals.publishing.umich.edu/ergo/article/7132/galley/4523/view/), pp. 26–27: the objection to Hakkarainen invokes Hume's “modest but positive science of human nature”. In context, Zahn argues that basic object beliefs belong to the philosophical disposition, while making their existence itself a topic of reflection moves into a sceptical disposition. His overall ambition is to establish a viable interpretation, not to demonstrate that all alternatives fail (p. 2). The issue is an attribution of commitments, not merely whether a philosophical system is logically possible.

Jani Hakkarainen, *Hume's Scepticism and Realism*, *British Journal for the History of Philosophy* 20(2) (2012), 283–309, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/09608788.2012.664024), was read in its complete 47-page [author manuscript](https://scispace.com/pdf/hume-s-scepticism-and-realism-3iyqczay45.pdf). Manuscript pp. 33–34 and n.35, p. 47 explicitly preserve empirical inquiry while suspending a particular metaphysical commitment. His restriction concerns perception-independent entities, not all belief or all philosophy. The note sketches an empirical standard but does not supply its full defence. ## Three attitudes that the argument must distinguish

The following is HPA's analytical vocabulary, not attributed terminology:

- **E:** empirical reasoning about the order, coherence and variation of experience.
- **O:** ordinary object judgments or stable-object presuppositions used in inquiry.
- **R:** reflective assent to the continued existence of perception-independent objects.

Let S be Hume's constructive science. The inference S → E is uncontroversial here. S → O is strongly supported by Hume's practice. Neither yields S → R without an additional premise about the content and force of O. Conversely, withholding R does not by logic alone warrant E. These two missing inferences are the respective burdens of the opponents.

## Primary evidence, with chronology

1. [T1.3.5.2](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/3/5), SBN84, leaves the ultimate origin of sense impressions unsettled among object, mind and divine cause, then permits inference from their coherence whether they are true or false. The full section explains causal reasoning. This is direct evidence that a constructive explanatory step can proceed before the metaphysical issue is settled. It is **not** a proof of all such inferences' epistemic warrant. A short public-text fragment was matched against the private preserved TEI witness; [receipt](../notes/primary-witness-T1352.json). This is exact wording corroboration, not full critical collation.
2. [T1.2.5.25–26](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/2/5), SBN63–64, restricts the explanatory undertaking to perceptions and experienced connections. The associated n.12, SBN638–39, strengthens the emphasis on appearances. **The note belongs to the 1740 Appendix**: distinguish its later clarification from the 1739 body text. Ignorance of intrinsic nature does not establish suspension about existence.
3. [T1.4.2.1](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/2), SBN187, says bodies must be taken for granted in reasoning. This is the strongest contrary evidence. The subsequent .3–11 separates what senses present from belief in continued and independent existence; .14 rejects an inference from perceptions to external objects. It is therefore necessary to explain *which* taking-for-granted is required, not to assume that all object judgments already amount to R.
4. [T1.4.7.4 and .11–15](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/7), SBN265–66,270–74, constrains both sides. Hume worries about successive assent to conflicting principles, then returns to philosophy with diffidence about scepticism itself. Temporal alternation is not automatically a solution; nor does returning to inquiry by itself specify the metaphysical content of the returning assent.
5. [E12.6–16 and .24–25](https://davidhume.org/texts/e/12) supplies later tests of sense correction, external-world belief and mitigated scepticism. It is not used to settle the Treatise by retrospective stipulation. The public edition's later copytext and the Treatise's earlier sequence remain separate witnesses.

## Explicit demonstration

**P1 — shared:** Hume undertakes and resumes a positive inquiry into human nature.

**P2 — textual:** at T1.3.5.2 he expressly allows an inferential investigation to continue while the ultimate origin of its perceptual materials remains undetermined.

**P3 — interpretive, limited:** this permission demonstrates explanatory independence at that local step. Extending it to the entire science or its epistemic authority requires additional evidence.

**P4 — logical:** explanatory independence and metaphysical suspension are compatible; explanatory independence and natural realism are also compatible. Neither actual attitude follows simply from the independence.

**P5 — contested bridge:** the commitments necessary for S include reflective endorsement R, rather than only E and O.

**Inference:** P1 does not establish P5. P2–P4 give a textually grounded reason to demand that bridge. Thus the existence of S cannot, without further argument, exclude Hakkarainen's restricted suspension. This is stronger than a bare conceivable countermodel because Hume explicitly licenses a relevant local methodological separation.

**Precise conclusion:** one attempted exclusion of the suspension option is under-supported if its decisive premise is merely that positive science exists. Zahn's fuller disposition account may still supply the missing premise. We have not demonstrated that it cannot.

## Strongest defence and response

**Defence:** Zahn need not assert a logical entailment. He can argue that Hume actually conducts science with natural object belief; T1.4.2.1 and ordinary scientific practice supply the relevant historical evidence. T1.3.5.2 brackets certainty about origins, not belief in bodies. A thinker can lack a metaphysical proof and still assent. Furthermore, an explanatory theory of inference is not an epistemology justifying its products.

**Response:** this defeats an attempted proof of Hakkarainen from methodological bracketing, and that stronger proof is withdrawn. It does not make restricted suspension incompatible with scientific work. The remaining historical contest concerns the content, mode and context of assent. A defence of Zahn must show why the natural commitment is reflectively retained in the relevant philosophical inquiry. A defence of Hakkarainen must show why the arguments for suspension govern this inquiry while its empirical judgments remain warranted. Neither is discharged by renaming the perspectives or counting them.

**Further pressure:** human science draws on observed conduct, communication and other minds (TIntroduction10; [T2.1.11.3–5](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/2/1/11), SBN317–318). The latter .4 expressly invokes causal inference from external signs to real existence. Explanatory independence is therefore not automatically evidential independence. If these warranted judgments assert other subjects' existence independently of the observer, and those subjects belong to the disputed class of external objects, existential realism follows for that class. Calling the judgments particular cannot block existential generalization. A defence of suspension must explain their content and warrant, rather than merely redescribe other people as perceptions; that substitution may change the explanandum. Independence from this observer also differs from independence from every mind or from non-perceptual material existence. This is a concrete limit on extending the local result.

The separate assessor verified a small citation correction: Demeter §2's structural-uniformity reference T2.2.11.5 should be T2.1.11.5. The correction locates the relevant evidence; it is not a consequential refutation of Demeter.

## Positive reading and substantial consequence

Read T1.3.5.2 as specifying the explanatory job: trace relations among impressions, ideas and belief without first resolving what ultimately produces sensations. Read T1.4.2 as a subsequent inquiry into a commitment the practice of reasoning ordinarily carries. On this reconstruction, the question whether external objects must be assumed in practice is distinct from whether philosophical examination vindicates or suspends assent to them. This explains why a theory of cognition can succeed locally while the external-world dispute remains open.

The consequence is a changed burden of proof in the realism/suspension dispute. Scientific productivity cannot itself decide it. The decisive comparison must specify one proposition, one context and one attitude. For example: after considering the second profound argument, does the philosopher *believe that the causes of perceptions continue independently*, merely *proceed with stable-object assumptions*, or *withhold that judgment while investigating perceptual relations*? Each reading must explain this same case and T1.4.2.1.

## Existing scholarship and novelty control

Tamas Demeter's [*Hume's methodological solipsism*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11098-025-02334-8), *Philosophical Studies* 182 (2025), 1467–1493, §§2 and5, provides an important existing methodological reconstruction. It uses the same T1.3.5.2/T1.2.5.26 combination. It distinguishes psychological explanation from extra-mental implementation and allows materialist sympathies. Its use of Garrett's genealogy of relative ideas also prevents treating it as a wholesale endorsement of Hakkarainen. The complete original HTML, notes and bibliography were read; exact printed locators for the decisive discussion are pp. 1470–72, verified in extracted publisher PDF text.

This is an **HPA comparison between arguments**, not evidence of a published Demeter–Zahn exchange. Demeter is a precedent for the methodological move, not an authority certifying our conclusion. Hakkarainen himself already makes the compatibility proposal in n.35. Accordingly, the present result supports and sharpens an existing position; it is not an original discovery of the relevant passages. Searches and backward references covered these originals, Zahn's earlier normativity article and Garrett's reply to Loeb. There is no exhaustive forward-citation audit, and no promotion to original intervention.

## Objection history and next discriminant

- **v1:** scientific truth assertions defeat Zahn's perspectivalism. Withdrawn: Zahn explicitly accommodates first-order scientific truth at pp. 14–16.
- **v2:** positive science is compatible with restricted metaphysical suspension. Retained, but compatibility alone does not attribute suspension to Hume.
- **v3:** T1.3.5.2 supplies a local textual basis for the compatibility claim. Retained after separate defence; explanatory permission distinguished from epistemic entitlement.
- **v4:** Demeter's existing reconstruction defeats novelty of the methodological move and adds a serious rival: bracketing plus natural assent. Final verdict remains a strengthened contestable alternative.
