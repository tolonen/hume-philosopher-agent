# HPA-007 — Hakkarainen, Baxter, and the objects of causal inquiry

12 October 2026. **Verdict: contestable integration; no reconciliation or refutation of the complete rival ontologies.** This adds constitution, identity and assent to HPA-006's account of causal attribution. It modifies the scope of that account rather than appending two names to its bibliography.

## The actual scholarly disagreement

Hakkarainen's *A Third Type of Distinction in the Treatise* (2012), fully assessed in HPA-005, permits whole–part dependence without treating the relata as really distinct in the sense governing unrestricted separability. His *Hume on Spatial Properties* (2015), manuscript pp2,12–18, develops a positive account of spatial properties as complex particular qualities. Its n13 explicitly distinguishes unity at a time from identity over time: this distinction is an existing resource, not our discovery. The 2019 *Unity of Determinations of Extension* remains unassessed in full after failed original-source routes; the 2015 argument cannot substitute for it. [Institutional 2015 manuscript](https://trepo.tuni.fi/bitstream/10024/101369/1/hume_on_spatial_properties.pdf).

Baxter's *Hume on Substance: A Critique of Locke*, penultimate manuscript pp25–26, directly answers Hakkarainen2012. His proposal is that a whole is “nothing but the many parts, perhaps in some relation.” Apparent asymmetric dependence then concerns collective existence, not dependence between two strictly existing things. His chapter already addresses the real compound idea and mass passages; citing them is not a new refutation. The full 27-page manuscript was read by a separate assessor, and root inspected pp22–27. [Original manuscript](https://philarchive.org/archive/BAXHOS-4).

The difference is not removed by saying that Hakkarainen discusses simultaneous unity while Baxter discusses persistence. Baxter's argument also concerns simultaneous composition. Nor should Baxter's own contemporary theory of composition as identity be substituted for his interpretation of Hume. Exact qualifications, argument sequence and access history are retained in the separate reading notes.

## What needs to be added to HPA-006

The previous dossier distinguishes a supported causal claim from an unsupported substance supplement. But **what counts as the subject of the claim** is a further issue. If we say that a body changes a person's thought, are the body and person genuine complex unities, related pluralities, or convenient objects of ordinary discourse? Success in predicting the change does not by itself answer that question.

The integration must therefore distinguish four questions:

| Question | Relevant evidence | Unlicensed further conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| What features and relations are presented? | T1.1.7.3/.17–18; T1.2.3.4–5/.13–16 | Every presented feature is a separate bearer, or every represented whole is a further entity. |
| What makes these parts one complex? | T1.2.2.3; T1.4.5.5; Hakkarainen–Baxter disagreement | Causal interaction alone supplies numerical unity. |
| What sustains an identity attribution through change? | T1.4.6.6–22; Appendix10–21 | Ordinary continuity establishes strict identity or a simple enduring subject. |
| With what kind of assent is the claim accepted? | T1.4.1; T1.4.7; Baxter's assent argument | Affirmative or normative content proves rational endorsement. |

These questions interact, but neither their answers nor their supporting evidence can be exchanged without argument.

## Primary evidence: organization without an additional bearer

[T1.1.7.3 and .17–18](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/1/7), SBN18–19,24–25, makes the exact length inseparable from the line and distinguishes figure from coloured body by comparisons with other examples. The distinction of reason does not first peel off a free-standing shape and then seek a substance in which to insert it. Hume's white globe, black globe and white cube show how different comparisons can select different aspects of what is presented together.

This supplies positive content to the earlier substance restriction. A feature can be available for comparison without being an independently existing additional constituent. Conversely, the possibility of selective consideration does not establish an independent entity corresponding to every expression. The exact ontology of the extended complex remains contested: that is where the rival interpretations enter.

[T1.2.3.4–5 and .13–16](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/2/3), SBN34,38–39, describes disposed sensible points and calls the compound idea real. A separated point is real but not extended. This is evidence that the manner of arrangement matters to what is presented. Yet it does not alone decide whether the real complex is one further entity or the parts collectively arranged. Baxter has considered this evidence; a successful objection must defeat his plural interpretation, not simply repeat the word *real*.

The strongest countertext to an easy realist conclusion is [T1.2.2.3](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/2/2), SBN30–31: the Malezieu argument contrasts genuine indivisible unities with a fictitious denomination applied to a number. Its role in attacking infinite divisibility must be retained. The text gives the nihilist an actual argument, not merely silence about wholes. Whether its relevant scope embraces every finite organized complex remains a disputed interpretive premise.

The resulting positive interpretation is limited but useful: inquiry can distinguish arrangements and their consequences without adding a substratum as an extra presented item. It must not conclude, solely from that success, either that complex unities exist or that they do not. This is an evidential limitation on that particular inference, not a claim that every form of metaphysics is meaningless.

## A genuine fork in the interpretation

Let a and b be distinguishable constituents and W their proposed whole. Both readings can allow a without b and deny the existence of the same arrangement after removing b. They differ over how to understand W.

On the realist reconstruction, W has constituents and depends on them. One must restrict the scope of the principle connecting distinction with independent existence so that W's dependence is not a contradiction. On the plural reconstruction, talk of W concerns a and b collectively; no further strictly existing W is admitted. Removing b prevents their collective existence without introducing a dependent entity.

**Interpretive premise at issue:** does Hume's independence claim at T1.4.5.5 quantify over every conceived complex as a distinct entity, or does the individuation of what is really distinct already exclude whole–part cases? Assuming either answer settles the dispute in advance. Formal consistency alone cannot determine which interpretation best accounts for Hume's words.

Suppose a visible arrangement changes when a constituent moves or disappears. A causal account can predict a changed appearance or response under either interpretation. This is a concrete reason that predictive success does not independently discriminate the ontological alternatives. We would need a passage or argument that establishes the relevant unit of existence, not simply further observations of the same change. The published whole–part reply therefore qualifies HPA-005: its local repair remains viable, but the nihilist has an articulated alternative, not a neglected counterexample.

## Identity and the person: stronger evidence, stricter limits

The complete [T1.4.6](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/6) distinguishes strict identity from the ordinary identity attributed to changing systems. Paragraph8, SBN255–256, supposes a connected mass whose parts remain uninterruptedly and invariably the same; motion or relocation does not by itself destroy the identity considered there. Adding or removing a part does. This is a conditional case, not an independent proof that actual composite entities exist.

Paragraphs11–14, SBN257–258, explain identity attributions to ships, organisms, rebuilt churches and rivers through purpose, reciprocal dependence and expected change. They do not silently repeal the strict criterion. Paragraph22 separately concerns the attribution of perfect simplicity to coexistent related parts. Denying such simplicity is not automatically a denial of every organized plurality or complex.

Paragraph19, SBN261, gives a particularly important positive model: perceptions mutually produce, destroy, influence and modify each other, and the mind is compared with a republic. It expressly allows the person to change character and disposition. The latter wording matches the private TEI witness exactly in `notes/primary-character-identity-v4.json`.

This corrects an incompleteness in HPA-006. **Stable character constrains an attribution within an empirically tracked personal history; it does not establish the identity of that person.** Otherwise we would assume that acts belong to one subject because they exhibit a trait, then use the grouped acts to prove the trait. Moreover, Hume's own character-change example shows that stable character is not necessary to ordinary personal identity. Similar character in two people is not sufficient either.

The positive extension is to make those dependencies explicit. In ordinary inquiry, an episode is located in a defeasibly tracked bodily and personal history; conduct and context then support or defeat a particular attribution. Tracking is revisable and need not posit an unchanging soul. But it is not a demonstration from a solitary perception that another person exists. The causal account remains local rather than pretending to found every condition of its own application.

## Why this does not solve the Appendix

The [1740 Appendix10–21](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/app), SBN633–636, is a later qualification of the 1739 exposition. It retains the distinctness of perceptions and the rejection of a simple substantial self, yet declares the account of their union in thought or consciousness defective. It does not declare every future solution impossible. It does prohibit presenting T1.4.6.19 as Hume's unqualified, completed solution.

There is also a direct problem with our own proposed extension. If causal connectedness were sufficient to constitute one person, sympathetic interaction between people could join their experiences into a single mind. A discriminating account needs more than a causal connection: it must identify the kind and boundaries of organization that belong to one history. Calling the result a *system* does not provide that account. This is our test of the reconstruction, not a newly attributed diagnosis of the Appendix or a novelty claim about the longstanding problem of mental unity.

A spatial account cannot simply fill the gap. T1.4.6.17 drops contiguity from its explanation of personal identity; a theory of spatially arranged points is therefore not automatically a theory of the unity of successive consciousness. This is where the proposed integration must stop rather than manufacture a reconciliation.

## Baxter on assent changes the status of our conclusion

Root read all26 pages, including notes and references, of Baxter's *A Pyrrhonian Interpretation of Hume on Assent*. The manuscript announces publication as forthcoming2016; the volume appeared2018. At MS17–18 he treats causal rules as “ultimately practical standards”; MS20 states, “Theoretical content does not entail active endorsement.” His account includes normative propositions and strong passive assent. It therefore already accommodates the affirmative scientific language emphasized in HPA-006. [Original manuscript](https://philpapers.org/archive/BAXAIS.pdf).

The consequence for our argument is exact. Hume's saying that motion causes thought establishes the assertion's content and its place in an argument. It does not alone establish that he endorses it in Baxter's contested sense. Nor does an empirical rule's capacity to distinguish better from worse attributions settle whether its authority is ultimately epistemic, practical, or local to resumed inquiry.

An objection to Baxter would need to show why his reading of the sceptical regress and its relation to the causal rules fails. Merely pointing to positive assertions or words such as *ought* is inadequate; his theory explicitly includes them. The root's complete reading identifies the relevant original disagreement with Garrett, but Garrett's1997/2007 treatments have not been newly read in full here. We retain a real dispute rather than awarding victory through an unassessed comparison.

## Result and consequence

Hakkarainen adds a positive question about the constitution of perceptual complexes; Baxter prevents that constitution from being inferred merely from ordinary whole-talk and prevents endorsement from being inferred merely from assertion. Integrating them makes the earlier account more precise:

**Empirical inquiry can discriminate causal relations and revise attributions without first supplying a substratum theory; nevertheless it employs units whose constitution, persistence and mode of acceptance require separate arguments.** Some empirical procedures may survive both rival ontologies. That compatibility is useful because it identifies what the procedures do not decide.

This gives a better research problem than a forced choice between metaphysical dogmatism and the abandonment of science. It also narrows our result: no demonstrated composition realism, nihilism, solution of the Appendix, actual universal suspension, or defeat of Baxter's assent account. The unity/identity distinction is already in Hakkarainen; the principal textual objections are already considered by Baxter. The integration is a candidate explanation of their bearing on HPA-006, not a certified original intervention.
