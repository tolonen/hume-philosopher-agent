# HPA-006 — Causal commitment without a theory of substance

12 October 2026. **Verdict: contestable positive reconstruction, with strong local textual support.** This advances the other-minds problem left by HPA-003. It does not promote a new scholarly discovery, establish actual universal suspension, or refute perspectivalism.

## The problem and the proposed solution

How can Hume investigate bodies, passions, intentions and other people while criticizing the philosophical grounds for belief in external objects? A familiar choice is to make science depend on metaphysical realism, or to explain its survival through changes between sceptical and ordinary standpoints. The published positions are subtler than this choice: Hakkarainen's restricted suspension concerns a specified kind of perception-independent entity; Zahn's perspectivalism allows first-order science. HPA-003 preserves their exact qualifications and source locations. Neither author should be assigned the crude alternative merely to make a third option attractive.

The positive proposal is that **Hume can discriminate between warranted and unwarranted additions to a causal claim within a single inquiry**. Evidence can support a real cause and a further prediction without determining the substance in which the cause supposedly inheres. But it supports each addition only through relevant experience. The distinction concerns the content of a conclusion, not only the strength of belief or the standpoint from which someone considers it.

This is more than permission to carry on despite ignorance. Hume explicitly affirms causal conclusions after separating the questions that cannot be answered. His own examples also show where a further attribution becomes an error. The resulting account gives positive human science a determinate procedure: establish experienced relations; compare relevant kinds; use independent evidence for additional attributes; correct the attribution when circumstances defeat it. It does not furnish a presupposition-free foundation for that procedure.

## The decisive evidence

**Substance and cause are different questions.** The complete argument of [T1.4.5](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/5), SBN232–251, supplies the main hinge. Paragraphs2–6 challenge the intelligibility of substance and inhesion. Paragraphs7–14 consider a different issue, local conjunction. Paragraphs17–28 include a reductio concerning Spinoza and the immaterialists; not every proposition entertained there is Hume's commitment. Then paragraphs29–33 turn to causation.

At T1.4.5.30, SBN247–248, Hume says that “we must separate the question concerning the substance of the mind from that concerning the cause of its thought.” He appeals to the experienced effects of bodily dispositions and affirms that motion actually causes thought and perception. Paragraph33, SBN250, repeats the positive conclusion, expressly limited to the causal relation of which we have a notion. Paragraphs34–35 retain the religious and immortality context; the passage is not a free-standing contemporary materialist manifesto.

The force of the passage is its conjunction: rejection of the supposed substance question accompanies a positive causal assertion. Merely pointing to Hume's use of bodily causes cannot therefore establish that he has quietly answered the substance question after all. Conversely, the passage rules out an attempted solution which admits mental causes but forbids material causes as such. **That first version of our reconstruction is withdrawn.** The decisive separation sentence has an exact literal match in the private TEI witness, recorded in `notes/primary-substance-cause-v3.json`; the complete paragraph is frozen privately. This is fragment verification, not full edition collation.

**One warranted relation need not license another.** T1.4.5.11–14, SBN236–239, supplies a concrete diagnosis. Taste and visible/tangible qualities exhibit causal and temporal relations. The imagination adds spatial conjunction to complete their union. Hume rejects this addition without discarding the experienced relations. This matters because even a proposed *relation*, rather than an intrinsic essence, can be an unwarranted addition. The proposed rule cannot simply be that relations are knowable and intrinsic natures are not.

**Independent evidence licenses further consequences.** In [EHU11.24–27](https://davidhume.org/texts/e/11), SBN142–147, the narrator challenges his friend with an unfinished building and a footprint: surely one can infer a cause and then further effects. The friend answers that familiar human producers belong to an independently experienced kind. Knowledge of human anatomy supports the second foot; experience of human purposes supports expectations about building. Those further conclusions do not come from the single initial effect alone. The purported divine cause lacks the same independently established background.

This distinction explains what an empirical inference adds and why it may add it. It also blocks a disguised repetition of evidence: infer a richly specified cause from one effect, then treat that enriched cause as independent support for additional effects. The narrator's final intervention, EHU11.30, SBN147–148, reinforces the requirement of experienced resemblance, although it raises a stronger question about any wholly singular cause. The friend's political conclusion is separately challenged at .28: what people ought to infer and what they actually believe are different matters. All these speaker roles must be preserved.

**Ultimate ignorance need not nullify explanation.** In [Dialogues4.13–14](https://davidhume.org/texts/d/4), KS163–164, Cleanthes denies that an explanation fails simply because we cannot explain the cause of its cause. Philo explicitly grants the legitimacy of explaining particular effects by general causes that ultimately remain unexplained. He disputes whether the particular divine system offers the relevant explanatory improvement. The shared concession supplies a positive criterion: general explanatory reach matters even where ultimate explanation ends. It does not make every proposed unseen cause acceptable, nor identify Philo's every statement with Hume's final view.

These are distinct arguments in works with different histories, not repeated versions of one isolated slogan. Their convergence supports a reconstruction; no unchanging doctrine across all textual revisions is presumed.

## Other minds as a worked test

The positive human-science case is unusually explicit. [T1.3.16.2–3](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/3/16), SBN176–177, begins with consciousness of our purposive actions and proceeds from similar outward actions in other creatures to similar internal operations. Later paragraphs test psychological explanations against learning. [T2.1.11.3–8](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/2/1/11), SBN317–320, distinguishes the idea of another affection, causal conviction and sympathetic conversion. [T3.3.1.4–7](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/3/3/1), SBN575–577, treats conduct as evidence of character and explicitly denies immediate access to another's passion.

The inference concerns an unobserved instance of an experienced kind. That differs from the specifically different material archetypes challenged in [T1.4.2.47](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/2), SBN212, where no observed perception–archetype conjunction supplies the required basis. The difference is not that one conclusion is unseen: both are. It is whether the proposed causal comparison has an empirical basis. The animal discussion supplies the explicit inference model; other *human* minds are the appropriate narrow test of Hakkarainen's human-perception-independence condition. Animal minds require a separate ontological classification rather than automatic inclusion under that condition.

Consider an observer who attributes pain from withdrawal near heat. Their own experience supplies a familiar causal pattern; intensity, avoidance, subsequent caution and surrounding circumstances can support or defeat the attribution. This example is ours, illustrating the textual procedure. A stage actor may reproduce a sign without the presumed feeling. Hume's own cases are stronger warnings: [T2.2.7.5–6](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/2/2/7) permits sympathetic affections corresponding to feelings that do not exist; [T2.2.9.13–14](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/2/2/9) discusses anticipated danger. The observer's resulting emotion is therefore not an automatic certificate of the other's present state.

This leaves a genuine realist commitment: another person's pain is a real event, not just a description of my current sensations. But a second human perceptual event is not thereby a nonperceptual substance independent of all human perceptions. Nor does ordinary bodily evidence automatically add such a substance. The distinction preserves empirical existential claims while requiring a further argument for the stronger metaphysical thesis. It cannot be used by a strict account on which only my present perceptions exist.

## Explicit inferential structure

1. **Textual premise:** Hume separates causal inquiry from mental-substance inquiry and makes a positive causal assertion after doing so (T1.4.5.30/.33).
2. **Textual premise:** he extends experienced causal comparisons to unobserved instances and tests the resulting explanations (T1.3.16); additional predictions can draw on independent knowledge of a kind (EHU11.25).
3. **Interpretive premise:** these passages identify operative standards within empirical inquiry, not merely descriptions of irresistible psychological transitions. Their argumentative use and explicit approval support this reading; unrestricted sceptical reflection remains a separate challenge.
4. **Interpretive premise:** another human mental episode can instantiate a familiar kind without identifying a material or immaterial bearer. First- and third-person access remain different; common kind does not erase that asymmetry.
5. **Logical step:** an existential claim about another human feeling does not by itself entail a claim about a nonperceptual bearer. Adding the latter requires a bridge premise. Calling both claims “realism” supplies no such premise.
6. **Conclusion:** positive human science has a textually supported form that does not first settle the disputed substance question. It can make and correct causal attributions while leaving that further question undetermined.

Here “does not entail” is a content distinction, not an empirical proof that the stronger thesis is false. As a schematic consistency illustration, multiple human perceptual events with regular relations can satisfy the weaker claim without including a specifically different bearer. This is not a demonstration that Hume endorses that ontology. His treatment of intelligibility also means that simply formalizing every disputed substance expression as a well-formed proposition would beg part of the question.

## The strongest replies

**Unknown substance and independent existence are different questions.** A realist may affirm perception-independent entities without possessing a theory of their substance. The substance/cause distinction alone therefore does not establish compatibility with Hakkarainen's exact suspension thesis. That further result depends on the reconstructed account of what the empirical claims entail, especially the distinction between another human perceptual event and an entity independent of all human perceptions. T1.4.5 supplies a key distinction, not the entire proof.

**Ordinary bodily realism has the same credentials.** Correct: a concealed clock mechanism or bodily process can instantiate an experienced causal kind. T1.4.5.30 is direct evidence. A realist who claims ordinary bodily commitments has not been refuted. The remaining dispute concerns whether those commitments establish Hakkarainen's stronger combination of ontological and causal human-perception independence, absolute externality and continued existence. The reconstruction contests an automatic transition, not every realist position or relative conception of an unknown cause.

**The other-minds transfer is not independently guaranteed.** Also correct. Many episodes in one's own mind are not many independently inspected minds. Successful behavioural prediction does not exclude an indistinguishable mindless alternative. Hume's analogy supplies defeasible empirical warrant; it does not demonstrate another consciousness against the radical sceptic. Testimony cannot remove that foundational difficulty by silently assuming the other speakers it was meant to establish. Separability of perceptions likewise does not remove the epistemic asymmetry. The solution concerns a local incompatibility alleged within empirical practice, not a complete solution of other-minds scepticism.

**A perspectivalist can retain these standards.** Yes. They can operate when inquiry resumes while losing authority under unrestricted reflection. Nothing here defeats T1.4.1's regress or proves continuous reflective assent through T1.4.7. Nevertheless, a change of standpoint is not needed to explain why, *within inquiry*, Hume accepts a causal claim and rejects its proposed substance supplement. This is the positive explanatory gain. It supports Hakkarainen against one science-based exclusion without deciding the entire Hakkarainen–Zahn disagreement. Hakkarainen already envisages compatibility, and Demeter already supplies methodological autonomy; the added work is an account of the content and correction of the positive inferences.

**Cleanthes's novel voice challenges a narrow experience rule.** [Dialogues3.2–10](https://davidhume.org/texts/d/3) imagines unprecedented intelligible speech from the clouds; Philo is reported embarrassed and gives no recorded reply before interruption. Our reconstructed answer permits inference from the familiar organization of speech despite its novel location and scale, without licensing infinity or a complete divine psychology. That answer concedes Cleanthes's point that repetition of the entire target situation is unnecessary. It is our answer, not a textual victory attributed to Philo. The cosmic case remains harder because the relevant similarity is contested.

**Part12 contains positive design assent.** [Dialogues12.2–8 and .33–34](https://davidhume.org/texts/d/12) must be retained, including strong formulations about an intelligent author and Pamphilus's preference for Cleanthes. The restricted final analogy cannot silently cancel every earlier affirmation. Our rule is compatible with some thin analogical assent, but does not settle all tensions between these statements and the singularity objection. Consequently it cannot establish universal religious suspension or Hume's personal religious belief.

## Scholarship, chronology and precise contribution

The basic own-case analogy is not new. The separate defender read **Byoungjae Kim, “Hume on the Problem of Other Minds,”** BJHP27(3)(2019),535–555, DOI10.1080/09608788.2018.1524365, in full; its accessible article pagination9–10 develops that inference. **Haruko Inoue, “Hume's Position Concerning Other Minds,”** Annals11(2)(2003),75–86, was also read in full and examines affection–behaviour types while defending a different person-perception account. These precedents prevent presenting analogy as the discovery. Complete Waldow, Pitson and relevant Garrett book treatments remain material comparison gaps. Ledgers distinguish original reading from abstract access.

The candidate contribution is the joint application of three independently evidenced distinctions—cause/substance, familiar kind/unobserved instance, and supported attribution/unsupported addition—to the current question about human science and restricted metaphysical suspension. It supplies a positive mechanism and corrections where bare compatibility would leave an explanatory debt. Its originality in scholarship remains unestablished.

Treatise Books1–2 appeared in1739, Book3 in1740. Hume Texts Online's edited Treatise includes errata and Appendix interventions. Its Enquiry follows1777, not an uncollated1748 first edition. The Dialogues witness is1779, with earlier composition and revisions; publication date alone cannot establish doctrinal development. Root also read the complete withdrawn essay “Of the Study of History” in public and2021Clarendon witnesses: its expansion of experience supports mediated inquiry, but neither narrative engagement nor withdrawal supplies an antisceptical proof. It is corroboration, not a load-bearing premise.

**Conclusion:** Hume need not purchase empirical explanation by answering the substance question. The texts offer positive, corrigible causal commitment whose content is limited by the evidence for each addition. This substantially answers the local challenge that human science must either settle metaphysical Realism or abandon its affirmative claims. It does not prove actual suspension, global justification, or priority for this reconstruction.

## Subsequent integration: Hakkarainen and Baxter

[HPA-007](HPA-007.md) qualifies this reconstruction in two ways. Character evidence works within an empirically tracked personal history; it does not establish strict identity or unified consciousness. Also, Baxter's fully read assent argument expressly permits positive theoretical and normative claims without active endorsement. The affirmative passages above therefore cannot by themselves defeat his account. “Warrant” here remains an interpretation of operative empirical standards, with their ultimate authority contested. Hakkarainen's positive spatial ontology and Baxter's rival treatment of wholes now supply distinct accounts of the entities such inquiry employs. The causal argument alone does not adjudicate them.
