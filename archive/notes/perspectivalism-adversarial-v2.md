# Separate adversarial assessment: perspectivalism and truth

Assessment date: 12 October 2026. This is a nonblinded automated assessment: the assessor received the proposed objection and was instructed to defend Zahn. It uses the inherited model; the exact model identifier and sampling configuration were not exposed. It is not an independent expert endorsement. No source payload was saved locally.

**Verdict:** withdraw the simple textual objection that Hume's scientific truth claims refute perspectivalism. Retain a narrower challenge to the inference from failed faculty vindication to the exclusion of a non-incidental connection with truth. That inference needs an additional premise. This supports keeping a naturalistic alternative in the competition; it does not establish Garrett's complete account, or refute perspectivalism.

## Source position and strongest defence

[Zahn, *Is Hume a Perspectivalist?*](https://journals.publishing.umich.edu/ergo/article/7132/galley/4523/view/), *Ergo* 12(1), 2025, pp. 1–34: the aim is viability, not victory over alternatives (p. 2). Truth-tracking is defined through covariance of justification and truth-probability (p. 3); the argument uses internalism and brackets externalism (p. 4 nn. 8–9). The strong defence grants scientific truth-conduciveness within a disposition while denying a standpoint-independent vindication (pp. 14–16). The title principle receives a practical interpretation (pp. 20–23). Epistemic comparison across dispositions is rejected (pp. 28–30); mixed dispositions are allowed (p. 33). The criticism of Hakkarainen concerns both domain division and external-object assent (pp. 26–27).

## The objection that fails

T 3.3.1.25 (SBN 588–89) explicitly links diversified evidence with increased assurance of a hypothesis's truth. T 1.3.15.2 (SBN 173) introduces rules for knowing when things really are causes and effects. Neither alone establishes that Hume regards scientific warrant as surviving every possible reflection on the faculty that produces it. We must not infer that universal conclusion from a local endorsement.

The decisive counterevidence to an easy naturalistic victory is T 1.4.7.3–9. Hume asks how to distinguish truth even if he finds it, connects judgment to imagination, sets out the dangerous dilemma, and describes a recovery through natural interruption. A reading that treats all of this as irrelevant to epistemic authority has not earned its result. T 1.4.1.8–12 additionally says that the same principles used to judge objects generate the sceptical regress when applied repeatedly to judgment itself. These passages explain why the higher-order challenge cannot simply be answered by producing one more ordinary successful inference.

## The precise inference requiring repair

The following distinctions are this assessor's analytical reconstruction, not terminology attributed to Hume:

1. **Actual connection:** a method produces a higher proportion of truths in specified circumstances.
2. **Accessible evidence:** a believer has experience, memories or other accessible grounds for expecting that connection.
3. **Independent vindication:** the method's connection with truth can be established without using the method, or any comparably disputed faculty.
4. **Constitution of warrant:** that connection is part of what makes a belief warranted, rather than a further property of an already warranted belief.

Failure at (3) does not entail failure at (1), (2), or (4). Equally, possession of (1) does not entail possession of (2), (3), or (4). The concept of internal accessibility does not, by itself, add a requirement that every accessible ground must receive an independent faculty-level certification. That stronger condition needs a historical argument.

This matters even without adopting externalism. Consider an explicitly hypothetical pair of worlds. In both, a subject has the same accessible observations and the same reflective corrections of rash generalisation; those mental facts are the entire criterion of warrant. In one world, the procedure continues systematically to predict events; in the other, the previously observed order ceases. The subject cannot independently exclude the second world. That inability does not show the first world's systematic success to be absent, nor that its causal explanation must be coincidence. The example establishes a non-entailment, not Hume's acceptance of either world or a solution to induction.

There is a strong response: perhaps the intended exclusion concerns (4), not (1). A truth connection that cannot function as an accessible, independently vindicated ground cannot be the ultimate explanation of warrant. On that reading, ordinary scientific success remains available and the objection about actual covariance misses the target. But this response relies on the disputed certification requirement. It also changes the relevant question from whether justification covaries with truth to what ultimately grounds justification. Those are distinct questions.

The appropriate verdict is consequently conditional. **If the conclusion concerns actual systematic covariance, the offered transition is insufficient. If it concerns an independently certified foundation of warrant, the narrower claim survives this objection, but does not exclude every naturalistic or fallibilist account of truth-oriented warrant.** Do not present this as a proven textual contradiction or as a decisive victory for Garrett.

## A positive reading to test

The strongest next hypothesis has three levels: causal explanation of inference; empirical correction of its applications; and the attempted vindication of the entire cognitive system. These should be compared without assuming in advance that the third level either destroys or certifies everything at the second.

T 1.3.6.4–12 explains why an appeal to experienced uniformity cannot independently produce its own presupposition. T 1.3.13.9–12 then distinguishes essential from accidental circumstances and corrects misleading similarity through more general rules. T 1.3.15.6–12 turns that practice into experimental discriminations, including explicit cautions against extrapolating from too few cases. These are substantial restrictions on warranted causal inference even though they supply no independent certification of induction as a whole. T 1.4.1.1–5 extends the same practice to fallibility of the reasoner. This sequence gives a naturalistic reconstruction positive material to explain; it cannot be dismissed merely because it does not satisfy the strongest grounding demand.

Its burden remains serious: explain why T 1.4.1.6 and T 1.4.7.7 do not give the unrestricted sceptical iteration equal or superior normative authority. T 1.4.7.11 may supply a restriction, but an argument must show why its authority is epistemic, practical, or both. Its placement among inclination, painful application and the return to philosophy prevents a bare appeal to the word *ought* from settling the issue.

## What the earlier original changes

The complete [*Two Forms of Doxastic Normativity*](https://humesociety.org/?hume_article_pdf=16453) was subsequently inspected: article pp. 3–22, PDF pp. 2–21. Zahn distinguishes epistemic assessment from practical permission, using the title principle and honest gentlemen (pp. 10–13, 17–18). The contested truth-tracking transition already occurs at p. 7 and n. 23, p. 21.

The following is this assessor's evaluation. The strongest defence must distinguish a reason to undertake or discontinue inquiry from a reason for accepting its conclusions as true. T 1.4.7.12–14 gives substantial primary support for the first category: inclination returns the philosopher to inquiry, while the honest gentlemen need not take it up. That is a genuine alternative to treating every favourable doxastic evaluation as epistemic. It makes the practical reading of the title principle a serious competitor. Rejecting it requires more than observing that Hume sometimes approves scientific reasoning.

Conversely, establishing two kinds of evaluation does not establish that epistemic warrant varies with disposition, or lacks a systematic relation to truth. A fallibilist could distinguish epistemic quality from whether pursuing a question is worth its psychological cost. This conceptual option does not settle how that fallibilist should interpret Hume's strongest sceptical passages. It shows that the case for two norms and the case for perspectival epistemic warrant require different arguments; the former cannot fill the missing premise in the latter.

There is also a precise, unresolved comparison to make. T 1.4.7.14 rejects acceptance merely because a hypothesis is agreeable; the same paragraph permits non-inquiry. These cases distinguish refraining from an investigation from positively adopting an unwarranted conclusion. Neither automatic epistemic priority nor automatic practical priority captures both without further specification. Any complete mixed account needs a conflict rule explaining why psychological cost can defeat some demands for inquiry while pleasantness cannot ordinarily legitimate bad inference. This is a burden for adjudication, not a demonstrated inconsistency: Hume need not have formulated a fully general decision procedure. The same burden applies to a naturalistic reconstruction that claims the title principle settles every conflict.

## A sharper primary-text discriminant

T 1.4.7.4 (SBN 265–66) asks whether successively assenting to incompatible principles permits philosophers to knowingly embrace a manifest contradiction. This means that temporal separation of assent is not automatically sufficient to answer Hume's own anxiety. A reconciliation must explain why this retrospective, cross-episode evaluation loses authority, or how the later position resolves it. It cannot simply assume that only simultaneously held beliefs are epistemically relevant.

The strongest answer is chronological within the argument: the passage is part of the crisis, and T 1.4.7.14–15 later treats philosophical doubts themselves with diffidence and permits conviction according to the current view of an object. That answer remains viable. What would discriminate between readings is a detailed account of what is retained from the crisis at the close, particularly the status of self-directed scepticism. This is a substantive interpretive test; it is not a newly established error.

## The Hakkarainen branch

After the publisher route failed, the parent supplied a [47-page author manuscript](https://scispace.com/pdf/hume-s-scepticism-and-realism-3iyqczay45.pdf). Its relevant original discussion was inspected directly, not through Zahn: PDF pp. 1–16, 20–21, 23–34 and notes 21–35. Published-page equivalence is not yet collated. Hakkarainen explicitly preserves inquiry while restricting suspension (PDF pp. 33–34, n. 35); n. 23 addresses temporal contradiction, and n. 32 leaves simultaneous domains open.

The necessary test is more precise than counting perspectives. Ask whether one and the same subject can conduct warranted empirical inquiry while suspending judgment on the metaphysical independence of its objects. Empirical investigation of perceptions does not logically entail commitment to mind-independent causes. An argument against suspension that relies on that entailment needs a further premise. Conversely, if scientific investigation necessarily presupposes external objects, distinguish its working presupposition from its reflective metaphysical endorsement. Mere silence cannot settle which attitude is present. The restricted-suspension option therefore survives the appeal to positive science. Its coherent availability does not establish its historical correctness: supplying the detailed account of empirical warrant remains an outstanding burden. This is a substantial issue about what scientific judgment presupposes, not merely how many domains to name.

The parent subsequently proposed two primary discriminants, independently read here. T 1.2.5.25–26 and its appended note 12 (SBN 63–64, 638–39) explicitly limit explanation to sensible effects and their connections. Their evidential role is to make an additional metaphysical commitment something to demonstrate. The strongest contrary reading is straightforward: ignorance of bodies' intrinsic nature is entirely compatible with confidence that bodies exist independently. These passages cannot by themselves prove suspension about existence.

T 1.3.5.2 (SBN 84) goes further by allowing inference from perceptual coherence while leaving impressions' ultimate origin undetermined among materially different possibilities. This supplies textual evidence against requiring metaphysical settlement before continuing Hume's positive account. But its immediate project is explaining causal reasoning. Permission to proceed with that explanation must not silently become a proof of the resulting inferences' epistemic warrant. Methodological bracketing is also compatible with an accompanying natural assent that the inquirer does not reflectively vindicate. This is the strongest residual reply; the passage narrows the dispute without resolving it.

Finally, distinguish a sensory judgment about a particular object, practical presupposition of stable objects, and reflective assent to perception-independent existence. T 1.4.2.3–8 and 11 explicitly separate sensory presentation from the origin of the belief in continued and independent existence. A reply that permits body beliefs without causal inference is consequently not yet a demonstration that senses alone supply the metaphysical commitment at issue. Equally, locating the belief's source in imagination does not itself show it is unwarranted. Each step needs its own argument.

A comparison must therefore put the original accounts into a common matrix: domain of inquiry, proposition considered, mode of assent, warrant condition, and temporal status. A difference in the number of labels is consequential only where it yields different predictions for one specified case. The Treatise and Enquiry must be treated separately before a cross-work synthesis is attempted.

## Final addendum: methodological autonomy and social evidence

Original access: [Demeter, *Hume's methodological solipsism*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11098-025-02334-8), *Philosophical Studies* 182 (2025), 1467–1493. This assessor read §§2 and 5 and n. 61 directly in publisher HTML, with contextual portions of §§1 and 6; this is partial coverage without printed-page collation. The account excludes extra-mental causes from psychological explanation while permitting materialist sympathies (§2), and incorporates a Garrett-based genealogy of relative ideas (§5, n. 61). Its comparison with Zahn is ours, not an identified published exchange or Demeter's endorsement of Hakkarainen.

The comparison supports a possibility, not an attribution: an explanation can be independent of a disputed metaphysical answer without its author suspending belief about that answer. Therefore this account helps resist an automatic inference from positive science to reflective metaphysical realism. It cannot establish Hume's actual suspension or supply the epistemic warrant for it. Likewise, explaining how a relative idea becomes believed neither affirms its referent nor proves that the explanatory investigator withholds assent.

The strongest further objection distinguishes **explanatory autonomy from evidential autonomy**. T Intro.10 (SBN xviii–xix) collects observations of people in social activity; it does not simply confine inquiry to undisturbed introspection. T 2.1.11.3–5 (SBN 317–18) describes inferring others' affections from external signs, invokes an inference to real existence, and compares the structure of minds with bodies. These give a realist substantial material: the investigator appears to treat other people and their unobserved mental states as sources of evidence. Removing physiological implementation from the explanation does not remove that commitment from its evidential basis.

The best reply must identify what those judgments assert. Observed expressions and actions can enter a description of perceptual regularities without independently settling their ultimate origin. But substituting the observer's experiences for facts about other subjects may change the explanandum, and cannot be treated as a cost-free paraphrase of Hume's science. If Hume's warranted social judgments explicitly assert other subjects' existence independently of the observer, and those subjects fall within the disputed category of external objects, existential realism follows for that category. Calling the judgments particular rather than general would not block that entailment. Whether that interpretation is required remains the precise question; independence from this observer must also not be conflated with independence from every mind or with non-perceptual material existence.

**Bounded outcome:** the comparison preserves restricted suspension as an option that positive science alone does not defeat, but supplies no verdict for Hakkarainen's complete position. Social evidence is the strongest outstanding pressure on a reconstruction that treats metaphysical non-commitment as effortless. It requires an account of warranted other-person attribution, not merely a declaration of methodological bracketing.

Locator correction: Demeter §2 cites T 2.2.11.5 for structural resemblance between minds and bodies. Direct checking locates that claim at **T 2.1.11.5, SBN 318**. T 2.2.11.5, SBN 395–96, concerns the relations composing amorous passion. The preceding T 2.1.11.4 supplies the more consequential real-existence passage. This is a narrow citation correction, not a refutation of the interpretation.
