## The strongest defence

Zimmerman distinguishes the causal target from the normative conclusion at p. 219. His reconstruction at p. 220 includes a premise limiting a faculty's powers by its function. At p. 221 he considers weakening the nonrepresentation premise. At p. 222 he acknowledges activities beyond proper function, then specifies a scientific restriction and immediately asserts that a distinct faculty is required for right action. The ensuing pages treat faculty individuation as an explanatory hypothesis supported by prior psychology, rather than a bare stipulation. At pp. 226–227 the account remains explicitly a reconstruction. These qualifications belong to his argument; a critique of an isolated modal phrase would miss them. [Original article, pp. 219–227](https://humesociety.org/uploads/issues/v33n2/383363.pdf).

The decisive primary evidence concerns argumentative purpose. T 3.1.1.8 does not merely introduce a free-standing account of rational criticism. It expressly recalls an argument offered to establish reason's inertness and promises to strengthen it for the moral case. The representation contrast immediately follows at .9. At .10 Hume states two advantages: a direct conclusion about moral conformity and an indirect conclusion employing immediate motivational incapacity. A reading that grants only the first advantage has to explain away Hume's express statement of the second. [T 3.1.1.8–10](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/3/1/1).

The earlier sequence points in the same direction. T 2.3.3.1 announces two causal theses. Paragraph .4 links them through contrary impulses and promises further confirmation. Paragraph .5 changes the immediate vocabulary to representation and contradiction, but that change does not establish a change of overall purpose. Paragraphs .6–.7 show how correcting a judgment can alter a dependent passion. The broader account already supplies a route from representation to conduct while denying reason a separate motivating impulse. [T 2.3.3.1–7](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/2/3/3).

Consequently, the initial objection's distinction between truth-evaluable contradiction and causal resistance is a useful analytical distinction, but not a counterexample to the causal purpose of the passage. It describes a possible inferential gap without showing that Hume intended no causal inference. Purpose, validity, and the best reconstruction must be evaluated separately.

## What this defence does not settle

T 3.1.1.10 specifically denies producing or preventing action **by contradicting or approving it**. It does not itself provide a general causal exclusion theorem for every effect that thinking might have. Paragraph .12 acknowledges judgment's influence through passion; .16 explicitly permits mediate causation. Treating inertness as the absence of every causal effect would therefore defeat the defence by misdescribing its target. [T 3.1.1.10–12, .16](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/3/1/1).

There remains a logical distinction between a faculty's failure to guarantee right action and its inability to generate volition without passion. The former does not entail the latter. But that elementary point cannot refute a reconstruction which also posits a constitutive limitation and appeals to a substantive account of psychological organization. A serious surviving objection must test those additional commitments against primary evidence or a better rival reconstruction. It must not attribute the weaker proposition alone to the complete argument.

One plausible alternative reads the representation discussion as confirming the limits of reason's government over passion while relying on the preceding causal psychology for independent motivational incapacity. This accepts Hume's retrospective causal purpose without demanding that .5 alone be a self-contained proof. The primary passages leave that option open; the present evidence does not uniquely establish faculty functionalism or establish that a rationalist should accept the proof.

The dossier's withdrawal is warranted. The residual issue is appropriately recorded as a research question about reconstruction, not a historical discovery, refutation, or novel objection. I make no independent novelty claim and have not checked the later literature beyond what the dossier reports.
