## Necessary correction

The assent section explains Garrett's semantic ascent and constitutive probability standard, but its reply then risks imposing a causal-content requirement on a position that explicitly rejects that requirement. In the complete original reply to Loeb, printed pp.293–296, Garrett treats sense and memory as noninferential sources of respectable factual belief. His composite-faculty account permits a faculty to contain a misleading sub-operation without thereby depriving every product of authority. The proposed report should state this alternative explicitly.

A suitable qualification is: the porter argument defeats the earlier HPA attempt to derive extra continuity from causal custom alone; it does not establish that every normatively reputable body-belief requires that derivation. Garrett's alternative instead needs an account of which content survives the criticism of the naive identification, and why the general existential content retains defeasible authority. HPA has identified this specific burden but has not shown that it cannot be met.

Without that qualification, the sentence about a constitutive standard failing to supply a missing content premise can read as a rejection by stipulation. The content could, on the rival account, arrive through another respectable source rather than as a missing causal premise. The real question is whether that source withstands its *particular* defeater, not whether it has an antecedent global vindication.

## Strongest remaining opposition

The positive method is useful but does not select a side in the Garrett/Baxter dispute. A reasoner can distinguish evidence for a narrated event from evidence for an author's conception under either theory of assent. Formalizing the target distinction makes the extension constraint explicit; it does not confer a new epistemic status on the discriminations. The report correctly acknowledges this limit and should retain it in all summaries.

Baxter's position must remain stronger than a denial of deliberate inquiry. His account allows choosing observations and comparisons while the resulting assent is causally produced. The objection to him therefore concerns the inference from involuntariness, plus the normative regress, to the absence of adequate epistemic endorsement. It cannot rely merely on the fact that Hume has an investigative method or says that an opinion is true. The report currently observes this constraint.

Conversely, granting a general naturalistic standard does not answer the ownership problem simply by citing better causal prediction. If episodes assigned to two people interact, connectedness alone does not identify one consciousness; stable character also fails to be necessary where a person's character changes. A rival can introduce further relations or criteria. The report is strongest when it rejects sufficiency of the stated premise and leaves those richer alternatives open.

## Scope and chronology check

The current report properly distinguishes 1739 Book1, the1740 Appendix, edited1777 Enquiry passages, uncollated1748 wording, and composition questions that publication chronology cannot settle. It also preserves affirmative theology as adverse evidence. Qu's established claim of increased practical regulation is acknowledged without being used as proof of a new epistemic foundation. The report should preserve these qualifications when condensed.
