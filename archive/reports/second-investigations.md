# Second investigations: arguments that discriminate between Hume interpretations

12 October 2026. This stage completes three additional bounded argument cycles, with separate automated defences of the scholars. It gives **qualified positive support to Garrett on induction and to Hakkarainen on whole/part dependence**, and strengthens the survival of restricted metaphysical suspension against one appeal to Hume's positive science. It does not establish a new decisive refutation or an original scholarly intervention.

The [dossiers](../dossiers/HPA-003.md), [separate assessments](../notes/garrett-induction-assessment-v2.md) and [source coverage](../SOURCES.md) expose the reasoning and its limits. The [dispute register](../DISPUTES.md) now follows identifiable disagreements and discriminating passages, rather than treating broad topic headings as investigations.

## 1. Garrett versus Millican: what custom explains

The useful question is not simply whether induction is psychological or epistemological. It is whether Hume's indispensable customary transition belongs to reason, and whether the distinction between reasoning and non-reasoning is the same as the distinction between epistemically good and bad processes.

There is a positive textual case for separating these boundaries. T1.3.6 assigns the relevant transition to imagination; T1.4.4 places it among stable and indispensable principles. If narrow imagination means only irregular or defective principles, those assignments become difficult to reconcile. A non-argumentative mechanism can be stable and useful. This supports a consequential feature of Garrett's interpretation.

The reflective, single-experiment case in T1.3.8.14 sharpens the result. Hume does not simply abandon custom when reflection is involved. He reinstates it indirectly through a habitually grounded general principle. This supports a dependence claim: even the reflective episode needs a customary basis. It does not show that an explicit argument can never help cause a prediction.

The strongest objection remains the persuasive failed demonstration. Showing that it is not a successful demonstration does not by itself show that its faulty step is outside reason. T1.4.1 and E5.22 give the objector substantial material. But the easy refutation fails: Garrett already permits bad probable reasoning, and his later reply explicitly handles the permanent/irregular distinction. The complete 2011 dialogue and 2014/2016 reply therefore changed the verdict in his favour.

**Result:** support Garrett's customary dependence and two-boundary account; retain a narrower challenge about faulty demonstrative performances and the identifying language of T1.4.7.7. This is a successful defence of an important part of an existing position, not a victory for Garrett's entire epistemology. [Full evidential chain: HPA-004](../dossiers/HPA-004.md).

## 2. Hakkarainen and metaphysics proper: which differences permit separation?

The central question is whether Hume's separability claims exclude all necessary dependence between numerically different things. Whole/part dependence is a decisive test because it concerns constitution, not hidden causal powers.

T1.2.3.13 asks us to separate a point from the *other points*. It does not ask us to preserve the identical extended whole without that point. An objection that moves from the first permission to the second changes the relata. This gives Hakkarainen's local repair a substantial defence: an element may exist without its whole even when the whole cannot exist without that element.

The defence has a genuine limit. Hume also uses universal-seeming separability language in T1.4.5.27 and T1.4.6.16. Contextual restriction is possible, but it must be argued. Excluding identical aspects from the principle's domain does not prove that only mutually independent entities belong to it.

Full reading of Clay reveals a more precise contest than unrestricted separability versus its abandonment. Hakkarainen's nonoverlap restriction differs from the noninclusion restriction Clay adopts from Qu. Two complexes can share a constituent while neither includes the other. W(a,b) can exist without V(b,c) when c is absent; V can exist without W when a is absent. Both repairs exclude whole/part pairs, but they differ on these overlapping complexes.

This is not an original counterexample: Qu already published it, and Clay expressly credits him. The reading corrected the initial novelty suggestion. A small finite check also confirms the known logical limit: conceptual separation's implying possible separation does not supply the converse. That check tests the formalized premises, not Hume's intended meaning.

**Result:** defend the local whole/part repair while withholding its exclusive generalization. Constitutive necessity remains compatible with denying necessary connections between appropriately distinct causal relata. This gives metaphysics proper a place in the project without forcing it into the New Hume binary. [Full evidential chain: HPA-005](../dossiers/HPA-005.md).

## 3. Hakkarainen, Zahn and the metaphysical commitments of human science

This is the strongest connecting investigation. Does the existence of Hume's constructive science itself rule out philosophical suspension about perception-independent objects?

T1.3.5.2 gives a direct reason to resist that exclusion. Hume proceeds with inference from coherent perceptions while leaving their ultimate origin undetermined. T1.2.5.25–26 offers related methodological evidence; its later Appendix note must be kept chronologically distinct. The positive reading is that Hume specifies an explanatory undertaking which can proceed before settling its materials' metaphysical source.

That supports the survival of Hakkarainen's restricted suspension option. It does not prove Hume actually suspends. A philosopher may bracket a question in an explanation while continuing to assent naturally to one answer. Nor does permission to explain an inferential process establish the epistemic warrant of its outputs. These were the strongest points in the separate defence of Zahn, and they constrain the final result.

Demeter's 2025 methodological reconstruction supplies an important published precedent for using the same passages. It also helps prevent a false alliance: methodological independence can coexist with materialist sympathies, and his account draws on Garrett's relative-idea genealogy. The comparison with Zahn is constructed here; it is not an identified published exchange between them.

Book 2 makes the contest harder and more useful. T2.1.11.3–5 discusses others' affections, external signs and inference to real existence. A human science can exclude physiology from its explanations yet still rely on other people for evidence. **Explanatory independence is not evidential independence.** If its warranted judgments assert independently existing persons, realism follows for the relevant class; calling these merely particular judgments would not avoid existential generalization. A defence of suspension must show how those social judgments work without changing their subject matter. A defence of realism must establish the disputed sense of independence and the mode of assent.

**Result:** positive science alone does not settle the realism/suspension dispute. The restricted option receives textual support against that exclusion, but its complete epistemology is unproved. The better decisive test is warranted other-person attribution across Books1 and2. [Full evidential chain and strongest reply: HPA-003](../dossiers/HPA-003.md).

## A related dispute now stated more precisely

The Zahn/Garrett comparison also requires distinguishing actual truth-conduciveness, accessible evidence for it, independent vindication of a faculty, and what constitutes warrant. Failure to obtain independent vindication does not by itself establish absence of a systematic truth connection. But a naturalistic opponent must still answer Hume's higher-order sceptical regress; citing ordinary scientific success does not discharge that burden.

Zahn's earlier distinction between practical and epistemic norms survives a simple objection. T1.4.7.14 permits people not to undertake philosophy while rejecting acceptance of an agreeable hypothesis merely because it is agreeable. Reasons to inquire and reasons to believe are different. Establishing that distinction does not by itself establish perspectival epistemic warrant. The [separate assessment](../notes/perspectivalism-adversarial-v2.md) records the conditional argument and its best defence; this branch is not counted as a fourth completed intervention.

## What was actually achieved

Nine additional scholarly arguments were read completely across the team: the Garrett–Millican dialogue; Garrett's replies to Millican and Loeb; Hakkarainen on scepticism/realism and on the third distinction; Zahn 2025 and his earlier two-norms article; Clay's manuscript; and Demeter's2025 article. Complete means original accessible text including notes and bibliography, not facsimile collation. Other relevant originals were read selectively or remain unassessed; the coverage table makes those boundaries explicit. Loeb's original critique is preserved but not fully read, so Garrett's reply alone is not treated as an adjudicated Garrett–Loeb exchange.

Ten additional source PDFs and extracted texts were preserved directly in private COMHIS Allas in this stage, including Zimmerman's formerly unresolved source. No local source PDFs or corpus payloads were retained. Hakkarainen's realism manuscript was read through the web tool but its durable download remains unresolved. The decisive T1.3.5.2 fragment was checked against the private primary witness; critical-edition and image collation remain incomplete.

The novelty checks had concrete effects. The bad-argument objection is established literature; the overlap example is already Qu's; the methodological bracketing argument has existing Hakkarainen and Demeter precedents. Automated agreement is not independent evidence, and these are development cases, not an uncontaminated evaluation set. The record therefore supports a claim of improved reconstruction and adjudication, not demonstrated original scholarship. The next substantive comparison should begin with Hakkarainen's unity-of-extension argument against composition nihilism and the original Baxter/Qu alternatives. Alongside it, the social-evidence test can connect Garrett's account of object belief, Hakkarainen's suspension and Book 2's sympathy. These choices follow specific unresolved premises. They do not reopen the first two withdrawn objections or start a new crawler or background run.
