# HPA-005: Hakkarainen, separability and constitutive dependence

Date: 12 October 2026. **Verdict: Hakkarainen's local whole/part repair survives; its exclusive general scope remains underdetermined.** The comparison with Clay yields a precise difference between two repairs. Qu's full argument remains unassessed.

## Exact claims and original coverage

Jani Hakkarainen, [*A Third Type of Distinction in the Treatise*](https://humesociety.org/?hume_article_pdf=16320), *Hume Studies*38(1)(2012),55–78, p. 56: “the principle holds only of really distinct entities”. All11 manuscript pages were read. Published-page mapping remains uncollated. Its presentation of Qu establishes Clay's adopted position; Qu's original full article was not obtained and is not silently treated as read.

## Primary evidence and the local defence

[T1.2.3.4–5 and .13–16](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/2/3), SBN34–35,38–39, describes extension through disposed sensible points, then separates a point from the **other points**. A single point presents no extension. It does not follow that the identical extended whole can exist without one of its requisite constituents.

**P1 — textual:** Hume permits an element to be considered apart from other elements.

**P2 — interpretive:** the very spatial whole under discussion requires those constituents in their relevant disposition.

**P3 — logical:** separability of a from the other elements b,c does not entail separability of the whole W(a,b,c) from a.

**Inference:** an objection that applies P1 to the whole/a pair changes the relata. It does not refute Hakkarainen's proposal that a whole and its part have asymmetric dependence. P2 still needs an individuation argument; no logical theorem establishes Hume's ontology.

**Positive reading:** Hume's point argument requires sensible elements and excludes extension made from nonentities. Separating the element leaves it real but unextended. This explains the sequence without attributing to Hume permission to preserve the identical whole while removing a constitutive part.

## Strongest counterevidence

[T1.4.5.27](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/5), SBN245–246, uses apparently universal language about perceptions and everything imaginable. The same section acknowledges extended perceptions and parts. [T1.4.6.16](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/6), SBN259–260, similarly treats the separability of mental constituents. These are real obstacles to a restrictive interpretation.

The strongest defence reads the relevant comparison classes in context: perceptions against alleged substance, or constituents against a supposed necessary union. This can explain why the immediate arguments concern distinct constituents rather than every whole/part pair. But contextual restriction must be demonstrated, not inserted whenever an unrestricted formulation is inconvenient.

The weak step is the proposed exclusion rule. Showing that separability does not apply to identical aspects does not establish that it applies **only** to mutually independent entities, still less that mutual independence requires no common parts. Thus the repair remains coherent while its claimed exclusive domain is not established by that exclusion alone. This is a local inferential limitation, not proof that Hume believed the unrestricted principle.

## A genuine disagreement between repairs

Let W have essential constituents a,b and V have essential constituents b,c. Hakkarainen's nonoverlap condition excludes the pair because they share b. The noninclusion condition adopted by Clay includes it because neither whole is part of the other.

| Comparison | Nonoverlap restriction | Noninclusion restriction | Existential separation under the stated model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole W(a,b) and part a | Excludes | Excludes | a can remain without W; W cannot remain without a |
| W(a,b) and V(b,c) | Excludes | Includes | W without V if c is absent; V without W if a is absent |
| a and c | Includes | Includes | Each can remain without the other |

This makes the dispute substantive. One-way or mutual existential independence does not require that every constituent can be removed while the other item persists. The overlapping case can satisfy mutual existential independence despite a shared constituent. It discriminates the two domain restrictions without deciding which Hume held.

**Novelty correction:** Qu already uses the overlapping-complex case on published p. 532, as explicitly reproduced and credited in Clay's manuscript p. 3. Its initial independent generation in this assessment is recorded, but it is not a discovery and does not prove absence of model prior exposure. Any suggestion that Qu or Clay missed the case is withdrawn.

## A second, formal scope check

Let D mean the relevant distinction, C mutual conceptual separation and P mutual possible separate existence. From D↔C and C→P one obtains D→P. One cannot infer P→D. The valuation D=false, C=false, P=true satisfies both premises and falsifies the converse. A finite check is retained in `scripts/check_argument_forms.py` and the private operational audit.

This reproduces Clay's manuscript p. 3 objection to deriving a biconditional from one-way conceivability. It does not establish that Hume lacks independent grounds for the converse, or that these symbols settle his meaning. It identifies exactly where additional textual evidence is required.

## Positive consequence and remaining work

Whole/part dependence supplies a serious route to constitutive necessity while preserving separation between genuinely distinct causal relata. Consequently, acceptance of constitutive dependence need not establish hidden causal powers, and denying such powers need not require unrestricted separability of every numerically different item. The metaphysical dispute cuts across the familiar regularity/realist binary rather than simply choosing one label.

Clay's manuscript p. 4 supports a further distinction: the [Enquiry4.9–11](https://davidhume.org/texts/e/4), SBN29–30, may retain separability reasoning with restricted causal distinctness. Continued use and unrestricted scope are different propositions. This is a later-text comparison; it does not silently determine the Treatise's earlier intention.
