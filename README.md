# Can an LLM contribute to Hume scholarship?

**This is an exploratory experiment to find out what current state-of-the-art LLMs can contribute to substantive Hume scholarship. The test took place on 12–13 October 2026.**

Mikko Tolonen gave an LLM a demanding research task, supplied access to existing open, publicly available resources, and subsequently clarified the scope and standards. The intention was to see what the LLM could investigate and argue independently, rather than develop an interpretation through intensive guidance from a Hume scholar.

## What we ask a philosopher to do

**[Read this short argument and answer three questions →](REVIEW_GUIDE.md)**

Allow about 10–15 minutes for an initial assessment. Send Mikko a short reply saying whether the argument is **correct, adds anything, and is useful**. The text contains the claim, its evidence, its strongest objection and the questions. That is the whole initial review task.

## What came out of the experiment?

The LLM proposed a general account, then found evidence that undermined its own stronger claim. Its surviving proposal is that Hume can support a limited empirical conclusion without thereby establishing the further existence, unity or authority attributed to it. We want to know whether this is a worthwhile philosophical result or merely a familiar distinction presented at length. Novelty and expert validation have not been established.

## Why run this test?

Recent LLM work in mathematics provides a point of comparison, but historical interpretation requires more than a valid proof. Philosopher-led LLMs may also become research tools. If science aims to advance truth, one can ask: “so what—why should the source of a sound contribution matter?” This experiment takes no side in that broader debate. It tests what the present system produced when given this task.
